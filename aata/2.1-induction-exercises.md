# The Integers — Mathematical Induction (Exercises)

Exercises for Judson §2.1, per `.claude/rules/aata-guide.md` — `std` used
liberally.

We reuse the arithmetic vocabulary of the main text (`aata/2.1-induction.md`),
importing the integer theory, its ring algebra, order/subtraction, the
nonnegative subclass with its upward-from-0 induction principle, and
divisibility with powers. Two of these exercises (5 and 9) are genuine
induction proofs; the rest are deferred (they need machinery outside bpa's
first-order integer kernel — summation operators, rationals, reals, factorials,
cardinality) or forwarded to the main text (the equivalence of the induction
principles).

## Exercises

**Exercise 1.** Prove that $1^2 + 2^2 + \cdots + n^2 = \dfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$
for $n \in \mathbb N$.

**Proved** (division-free ℤ form) — `std/integer-sum.bpa` `sumOfSquares`:
$6\sum_{i<n} i^2 = (n-1)\,n\,(2n-1)$ (reindexed to $i<n$). The classic
$\tfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$ is this multiplied through by $6$ — ℚ is **not** needed;
only the fractional *notation* would need it, not the theorem. Proved by
nonneg-induction over the identity-square sequence $\text{at}(i)=i^2$; the cubic
ring step closes by `polynomial`.

**Exercise 2.** Prove that $1^3 + 2^3 + \cdots + n^3 = \dfrac{n^2(n+1)^2}{4}$
for $n \in \mathbb N$.

**Proved** (division-free ℤ form) — `std/integer-sum.bpa` `sumOfCubes`:
$4\sum_{i<n} i^3 = (n(n-1))^2$ (reindexed). Same identity-sequence +
nonneg-induction pattern as Exercise 1; the quartic ring step closes by
`polynomial`. ℚ not needed.

**Exercise 3.** Prove that $n! > 2^n$ for $n \ge 4$.

**Deferred:** needs a `factorial` function and strict-inequality (`<`) induction
from a nonzero base $n_0 = 4$; deferred.

**Exercise 4.** Prove that $x + 4x + \cdots + (3n-2)x = \dfrac{n(3n-1)x}{2}$ for
$n \in \mathbb N$.

**Proved** (division-free ℤ form) — `std/integer-sum.bpa`
`sumOfArithmeticProgression`: $2\sum_{i<n} (3i+1)x = n\,(3n-1)\,x$ (the
progression $x, 4x, 7x, \dots$, 0-indexed term $(3i+1)x$, reindexed to $i<n$).
Judson's $\tfrac{n(3n-1)x}{2}$ is this over $2$ — ℚ not needed. Same pattern;
the ring step closes by `polynomial`.

**Exercise 5.** Prove that $10^{n+1} + 10^n + 1$ is divisible by $3$ for
$n \in \mathbb N$.

This is the divisibility-induction flavour of the main text's
`fourthPowerMinusOne`. Write $N(k) = 10^{k+1} + 10^k + 1$. The inductive step
rests on the integer identity $N(k+1) = 10\,N(k) - 9$: pushing each power up one
via $10^{m+1} = 10^m\cdot 10$ multiplies $N(k)$ by $10$, overshooting the
trailing $+1$ by $10 - 1 = 9$, which the $-9$ corrects. Since $3 \mid N(k)$
gives $3 \mid 10\,N(k)$ and $3 \mid 9$ (as $9 = 3\cdot 3$), $3$ divides the
difference $N(k+1)$.

First the base case at $n = 0$: $N(0) = 10^1 + 10^0 + 1 = 10 + 1 + 1 = 12 = 3\cdot 4$.

```bpa
theorem baseCaseDivisibleByThree:
  does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(ZERO)), pow(TEN, ZERO)), ONE))
proof
  @ten-to-the-first-unfolds |
    pow(TEN, succ(ZERO)) = mul(pow(TEN, ZERO), TEN)
    [using specialize powSucc(TEN, ZERO)]
  @ten-to-the-zero |
    pow(TEN, ZERO) = ONE
    [using specialize powZero(TEN)]
  @ten-to-the-first-is-ten |
    pow(TEN, succ(ZERO)) = TEN
    [using simplify ten-to-the-first-unfolds ten-to-the-zero mulOneLeft]
  @value-is-itself |
    add(add(pow(TEN, succ(ZERO)), pow(TEN, ZERO)), ONE) = add(add(pow(TEN, succ(ZERO)), pow(TEN, ZERO)), ONE)
    [by reflexivity]
  @value-with-first-power-as-ten |
    add(add(pow(TEN, succ(ZERO)), pow(TEN, ZERO)), ONE) = add(add(TEN, pow(TEN, ZERO)), ONE)
    [by rewrite ten-to-the-first-is-ten value-is-itself]
  @value-with-both-powers |
    add(add(pow(TEN, succ(ZERO)), pow(TEN, ZERO)), ONE) = add(add(TEN, ONE), ONE)
    [by rewrite ten-to-the-zero value-with-first-power-as-ten]
  @twelve-is-three-times-four |
    add(add(TEN, ONE), ONE) = mul(THREE, FOUR)
    [using simplify mulSuccRight mulZeroRight mulOneRight addZeroLeft addSuccLeft addSuccRight addZeroRight oneIsSuccZero]
  @value-is-twelve |
    add(add(pow(TEN, succ(ZERO)), pow(TEN, ZERO)), ONE) = mul(THREE, FOUR)
    [by rewrite twelve-is-three-times-four value-with-both-powers]
  @three-times-four-is-itself |
    mul(THREE, FOUR) = mul(THREE, FOUR)
    [by reflexivity]
  @three-divides-three-times-four |
    does_divide(THREE, mul(THREE, FOUR))
    [by exists_intro(FOUR) three-times-four-is-itself]
  @conclusion |
    does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(ZERO)), pow(TEN, ZERO)), ONE))
    [by rewrite value-is-twelve three-divides-three-times-four]
qed
```

The successor identity $N(k+1) = 10\,N(k) - 9$ is pure arithmetic. Written with
$q = 10^k$ (so $10^{k+1} = 10q$ and $10^{k+2} = 100q$), it reads
$100q + 10q + 1 = (10q + q + 1)\cdot 10 - 9$: a polynomial identity in $q$, whose
two sides both normalize to $110q + 1$. We prove this pure form once, over a
free $q$, letting `simplify` clear the ring rearrangement.

```bpa
theorem tenTimesMinusNineIdentity: forall q: Int;
  add(add(mul(mul(q, TEN), TEN), mul(q, TEN)), ONE)
  = sub(mul(add(add(mul(q, TEN), q), ONE), TEN), NINE)
proof
  @generalize-q |
    fix q: Int {
      @conclusion-ten-times-minus-nine-identity |
        add(add(mul(mul(q, TEN), TEN), mul(q, TEN)), ONE)
          = sub(mul(add(add(mul(q, TEN), q), ONE), TEN), NINE)
        [using simplify definitionOfSubtraction mulZeroRight mulSuccRight mulOneRight addZeroRight addZeroLeft addSuccRight addPrevRight addSuccLeft addPrevLeft negSucc negZero oneIsSuccZero succPrev prevSucc addIsAssociative]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall q: Int;
      add(add(mul(mul(q, TEN), TEN), mul(q, TEN)), ONE)
      = sub(mul(add(add(mul(q, TEN), q), ONE), TEN), NINE)
    [by forall_intro generalize-q]
qed
```

Substituting $q = 10^k$ and pushing the two powers up one via `powSucc`
($10^{k+1} = 10^k\cdot 10 = 10q$, $10^{k+2} = 10^{k+1}\cdot 10 = (10q)\cdot 10$)
turns that pure identity into the divisibility-shaped successor identity
$N(k+1) = 10\,N(k) - 9$ in the actual powers.

```bpa
theorem successorIsTenTimesMinusNine: forall k: Int;
  add(add(pow(TEN, succ(succ(k))), pow(TEN, succ(k))), ONE)
  = sub(mul(add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE), TEN), NINE)
proof
  @generalize-k |
    fix k: Int {
      @lower-power-is-ten-times-power |
        pow(TEN, succ(k)) = mul(pow(TEN, k), TEN)
        [using specialize powSucc(TEN, k)]
      @higher-power-is-ten-times-lower |
        pow(TEN, succ(succ(k))) = mul(pow(TEN, succ(k)), TEN)
        [using specialize powSucc(TEN, succ(k))]
      @higher-power-is-ten-times-ten-times-power |
        pow(TEN, succ(succ(k))) = mul(mul(pow(TEN, k), TEN), TEN)
        [by rewrite lower-power-is-ten-times-power higher-power-is-ten-times-lower]
      @identity-at-power |
        add(add(mul(mul(pow(TEN, k), TEN), TEN), mul(pow(TEN, k), TEN)), ONE)
          = sub(mul(add(add(mul(pow(TEN, k), TEN), pow(TEN, k)), ONE), TEN), NINE)
        [using specialize tenTimesMinusNineIdentity(pow(TEN, k))]
      @successor-value-in-powers |
        add(add(pow(TEN, succ(succ(k))), mul(pow(TEN, k), TEN)), ONE)
          = sub(mul(add(add(mul(pow(TEN, k), TEN), pow(TEN, k)), ONE), TEN), NINE)
        [by rewrite higher-power-is-ten-times-ten-times-power identity-at-power]
      @conclusion-successor-is-ten-times-minus-nine |
        add(add(pow(TEN, succ(succ(k))), pow(TEN, succ(k))), ONE)
          = sub(mul(add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE), TEN), NINE)
        [by rewrite lower-power-is-ten-times-power successor-value-in-powers]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall k: Int;
      add(add(pow(TEN, succ(succ(k))), pow(TEN, succ(k))), ONE)
      = sub(mul(add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE), TEN), NINE)
    [by forall_intro generalize-k]
qed
```

Now induction. The base case is `baseCaseDivisibleByThree`. For the step, from
$3 \mid N(k)$ we get $3 \mid 10\,N(k)$ (`dividesMul`) and $3 \mid 9$ (as
$9 = 3\cdot 3$), so $3 \mid 10\,N(k) - 9$ (`dividesSubtracts`), which the
identity equals $N(k+1)$.

```bpa
theorem tenPowerSumDivisibleByThree:
  forall n: Int; is_nonneg(n) ->
  does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(n)), pow(TEN, n)), ONE))
proof
  @induction-step |
    fix k: Int {
      @given-inductive-hypothesis |
        assume does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE)) {
          @inductive-hypothesis |
            does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE))
            [by hypothesis given-inductive-hypothesis]
          @three-divides-ten-times-value |
            does_divide(THREE, mul(add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE), TEN))
            [using specialize dividesMul(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE), TEN) inductive-hypothesis]
          @nine-is-three-times-three |
            NINE = mul(THREE, THREE)
            [using simplify mulSuccRight mulZeroRight mulOneRight addZeroLeft addSuccLeft addSuccRight addZeroRight oneIsSuccZero]
          @three-times-three-is-itself |
            mul(THREE, THREE) = mul(THREE, THREE)
            [by reflexivity]
          @three-divides-three-times-three |
            does_divide(THREE, mul(THREE, THREE))
            [by exists_intro(THREE) three-times-three-is-itself]
          @three-divides-nine |
            does_divide(THREE, NINE)
            [by rewrite nine-is-three-times-three three-divides-three-times-three]
          @three-divides-ten-times-value-minus-nine |
            does_divide(THREE, sub(mul(add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE), TEN), NINE))
            [using specialize dividesSubtracts(THREE, mul(add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE), TEN), NINE) three-divides-ten-times-value three-divides-nine]
          @successor-value-splits |
            add(add(pow(TEN, succ(succ(k))), pow(TEN, succ(k))), ONE)
              = sub(mul(add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE), TEN), NINE)
            [using specialize successorIsTenTimesMinusNine(k)]
          @three-divides-successor-value |
            does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(succ(k))), pow(TEN, succ(k))), ONE))
            [by rewrite successor-value-splits three-divides-ten-times-value-minus-nine]
        }
      @inductive-hypothesis-implies-successor |
        does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE))
          -> does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(succ(k))), pow(TEN, succ(k))), ONE))
        [by implies_intro given-inductive-hypothesis]
      @induction-step-at-k |
        is_nonneg(k) -> does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE))
          -> does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(succ(k))), pow(TEN, succ(k))), ONE))
        [using tautology inductive-hypothesis-implies-successor]
    }
  @induction-step-for-all-k |
    forall k: Int; is_nonneg(k) -> does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(k)), pow(TEN, k)), ONE))
      -> does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(succ(k))), pow(TEN, succ(k))), ONE))
    [by forall_intro induction-step]
  @base-case |
    does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(ZERO)), pow(TEN, ZERO)), ONE))
    [by theorem baseCaseDivisibleByThree]
  @conclusion |
    forall n: Int; is_nonneg(n) ->
      does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(n)), pow(TEN, n)), ONE))
    [using instantiation nonnegInduction((fun m: Int => does_divide(THREE, add(add(pow(TEN, succ(m)), pow(TEN, m)), ONE)))) base-case induction-step-for-all-k]
qed
```

**Exercise 9.** Use induction to prove that $1 + 2 + 2^2 + \cdots + 2^n =
2^{n+1} - 1$ for $n \in \mathbb N$.

We name the geometric sum by a local recursive function `geomSum`, with
$\operatorname{geomSum}(0) = 1$ and $\operatorname{geomSum}(k+1) =
\operatorname{geomSum}(k) + 2^{k+1}$, and prove $\operatorname{geomSum}(n) =
2^{n+1} - 1$ by induction. The inductive step turns on the doubling identity
$2^{k+1} + 2^{k+1} = 2^{k+1}\cdot 2 = 2^{k+2}$: the running sum $2^{k+1} - 1$
plus the new term $2^{k+1}$ is $2\cdot 2^{k+1} - 1 = 2^{k+2} - 1$.

```bpa
theorem doubling: forall a: Int; mul(a, TWO) = add(a, a)
proof
  @generalize-a |
    fix a: Int {
      @conclusion-doubling |
        mul(a, TWO) = add(a, a)
        [using simplify mulSuccRight mulOneRight]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall a: Int; mul(a, TWO) = add(a, a)
    [by forall_intro generalize-a]
qed

func geomSum(n: Int): Int
axiom geomZero: geomSum(ZERO) = ONE
axiom geomSucc: forall n: Int; geomSum(succ(n)) = add(geomSum(n), pow(TWO, succ(n)))
```

Base case: $\operatorname{geomSum}(0) = 1 = 2 - 1 = 2^1 - 1$.

```bpa
theorem baseCaseGeometricSum: geomSum(ZERO) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(ZERO)), ONE)
proof
  @two-to-the-first-unfolds |
    pow(TWO, succ(ZERO)) = mul(pow(TWO, ZERO), TWO)
    [using specialize powSucc(TWO, ZERO)]
  @two-to-the-zero |
    pow(TWO, ZERO) = ONE
    [using specialize powZero(TWO)]
  @two-to-the-first-is-two |
    pow(TWO, succ(ZERO)) = TWO
    [using simplify two-to-the-first-unfolds two-to-the-zero mulOneLeft]
  @two-minus-one-is-one |
    sub(TWO, ONE) = ONE
    [using arithmetic]
  @difference-of-power-is-one |
    sub(pow(TWO, succ(ZERO)), ONE) = ONE
    [by rewrite two-to-the-first-is-two two-minus-one-is-one]
  @geometric-sum-at-zero |
    geomSum(ZERO) = ONE
    [by axiom geomZero]
  @conclusion |
    geomSum(ZERO) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(ZERO)), ONE)
    [by rewrite difference-of-power-is-one geometric-sum-at-zero]
qed
```

The inductive step: assume $\operatorname{geomSum}(k) = 2^{k+1} - 1$; then
$\operatorname{geomSum}(k+1) = (2^{k+1} - 1) + 2^{k+1} = 2\cdot 2^{k+1} - 1 =
2^{k+2} - 1$.

```bpa
theorem geometricSumOfPowersOfTwo:
  forall n: Int; is_nonneg(n) -> geomSum(n) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(n)), ONE)
proof
  @induction-step |
    fix k: Int {
      @given-inductive-hypothesis |
        assume geomSum(k) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(k)), ONE) {
          @inductive-hypothesis |
            geomSum(k) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(k)), ONE)
            [by hypothesis given-inductive-hypothesis]
          @successor-sum-unfolds |
            geomSum(succ(k)) = add(geomSum(k), pow(TWO, succ(k)))
            [using specialize geomSucc(k)]
          @successor-sum-with-hypothesis |
            geomSum(succ(k)) = add(sub(pow(TWO, succ(k)), ONE), pow(TWO, succ(k)))
            [by rewrite inductive-hypothesis successor-sum-unfolds]
          @power-doubles |
            mul(pow(TWO, succ(k)), TWO) = add(pow(TWO, succ(k)), pow(TWO, succ(k)))
            [using specialize doubling(pow(TWO, succ(k)))]
          @two-copies-give-double-minus-one |
            add(sub(pow(TWO, succ(k)), ONE), pow(TWO, succ(k)))
              = sub(mul(pow(TWO, succ(k)), TWO), ONE)
            [using arithmetic power-doubles]
          @successor-sum-is-double-minus-one |
            geomSum(succ(k)) = sub(mul(pow(TWO, succ(k)), TWO), ONE)
            [using arithmetic two-copies-give-double-minus-one successor-sum-with-hypothesis]
          @next-power-unfolds |
            pow(TWO, succ(succ(k))) = mul(pow(TWO, succ(k)), TWO)
            [using specialize powSucc(TWO, succ(k))]
          @successor-sum-is-next-power-minus-one |
            geomSum(succ(k)) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(succ(k))), ONE)
            [by rewrite next-power-unfolds successor-sum-is-double-minus-one]
        }
      @hypothesis-implies-successor |
        geomSum(k) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(k)), ONE)
          -> geomSum(succ(k)) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(succ(k))), ONE)
        [by implies_intro given-inductive-hypothesis]
      @induction-step-at-k |
        is_nonneg(k) -> geomSum(k) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(k)), ONE)
          -> geomSum(succ(k)) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(succ(k))), ONE)
        [using tautology hypothesis-implies-successor]
    }
  @induction-step-for-all-k |
    forall k: Int; is_nonneg(k) -> geomSum(k) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(k)), ONE)
      -> geomSum(succ(k)) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(succ(k))), ONE)
    [by forall_intro induction-step]
  @base-case |
    geomSum(ZERO) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(ZERO)), ONE)
    [by theorem baseCaseGeometricSum]
  @conclusion |
    forall n: Int; is_nonneg(n) -> geomSum(n) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(n)), ONE)
    [using instantiation nonnegInduction((fun m: Int => geomSum(m) = sub(pow(TWO, succ(m)), ONE))) base-case induction-step-for-all-k]
qed
```
