# Irreducible Polynomials

A literate `2b4m` transliteration of **Chapter 17 ("Polynomials"), §17.3 "Irreducible
Polynomials"** of Thomas W. Judson's *Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications* (source:
`vendor/aata/src/poly.xml`, lines 683–1075; © 1997–2026 Judson, GFDL 1.3). Run
`2b4m check aata/17.3-irreducible-polynomials.md` to verify.

A nonconstant polynomial $f(x) \in F[x]$ is **irreducible** over a field $F$ if $f(x)$ cannot
be expressed as a product of two polynomials $g(x)$ and $h(x)$ in $F[x]$, where the degrees of
$g(x)$ and $h(x)$ are both smaller than the degree of $f(x)$. Irreducible polynomials function
as the prime numbers of polynomial rings.

```2b4m
import fieldpoly <<< "std/field/polynomial.b4m"
import peano <<< "std/peano.b4m"

sort Poly = fieldpoly.Poly
const ZEROP = fieldpoly.ZEROP
func add = fieldpoly.addP
func mul = fieldpoly.mulP
func degree = fieldpoly.degree
const ZERO = peano.ZERO
pred less_than = peano.less_than

// Judson's definition, verbatim: nonconstant, and not a product of two strictly
// lower-degree factors.
import polyirreducible <<< "std/field/irreducible.b4m"

pred isIrreducible = polyirreducible.isIrreducible
fact irreducibilityIsNoLowerDegreeFactorisation =
  polyirreducible.irreducibilityIsNoLowerDegreeFactorisation
```

> **Numerics are not in scope.** His three examples — $x^2 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, $x^2 + 1$
> over $\mathbb{R}$, and $x^3 + x^2 + 2$ over $\mathbb{Z}_3$ (checked by evaluating at $0$,
> $1$, $2$) — are numeric verifications over specific fields, and the chapter's convention
> leaves those untranslated. The general remark they illustrate is the definition above.

A first consequence of the definition, in the form Judson's $\mathbb{Z}_3$ example uses it: an
irreducible polynomial has no factorisation into two lower-degree pieces, so exhibiting one
refutes irreducibility.

```2b4m
theorem aFactorisationRefutesIrreducibility: forall f, g, h: Poly;
  isIrreducible(f) -> f = mul(g, h) ->
  (not (less_than(degree(g), degree(f)) and less_than(degree(h), degree(f))))
proof
  @generalize-f |
    fix f: Poly {
      @generalize-g |
        fix g: Poly {
          @generalize-h |
            fix h: Poly {
              @given-irreducible |
                assume isIrreducible(f) {
                  @irreducible |
                    isIrreducible(f)
                    [by hypothesis given-irreducible]
                  @the-definition |
                    isIrreducible(f) iff
                      (less_than(ZERO, degree(f)) and
                      (forall g2, h2: Poly;
                      f = mul(g2, h2) ->
                      (not (less_than(degree(g2), degree(f)) and
                      less_than(degree(h2), degree(f))))))
                    [using specialize irreducibilityIsNoLowerDegreeFactorisation(f)]
                  @the-two-conjuncts |
                    less_than(ZERO, degree(f)) and
                      (forall g2, h2: Poly;
                      f = mul(g2, h2) ->
                      (not (less_than(degree(g2), degree(f)) and
                      less_than(degree(h2), degree(f)))))
                    [using tautology the-definition irreducible]
                  // the definition's second conjunct is the no-factorisation clause
                  @the-no-factorisation-clause |
                    forall g2, h2: Poly;
                      f = mul(g2, h2) ->
                      (not (less_than(degree(g2), degree(f)) and
                      less_than(degree(h2), degree(f))))
                    [by and_rhs the-two-conjuncts]
                  @conclusion-at-these-factors |
                    f = mul(g, h) ->
                      (not (less_than(degree(g), degree(f)) and
                      less_than(degree(h), degree(f))))
                    [using specialize the-no-factorisation-clause(g, h)]
                }
              @irreducible-implies-no-factorisation |
                isIrreducible(f) -> f = mul(g, h) ->
                  (not (less_than(degree(g), degree(f)) and
                  less_than(degree(h), degree(f))))
                [by discharge given-irreducible]
            }
          @discharge-h |
            forall h9: Poly;
              isIrreducible(f) -> f = mul(g, h9) ->
              (not (less_than(degree(g), degree(f)) and
              less_than(degree(h9), degree(f))))
            [by generalize generalize-h]
        }
      @discharge-g |
        forall g9, h9: Poly;
          isIrreducible(f) -> f = mul(g9, h9) ->
          (not (less_than(degree(g9), degree(f)) and
          less_than(degree(h9), degree(f))))
        [by generalize generalize-g]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall f, g, h: Poly;
      isIrreducible(f) -> f = mul(g, h) ->
      (not (less_than(degree(g), degree(f)) and less_than(degree(h), degree(f))))
    [by generalize generalize-f]
qed
```

**Lemma 17.12** Let $p(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$. Then
$$p(x) = \frac{r}{s}(a_0 + a_1 x + \cdots + a_n x^n),$$
where $r, s, a_0, \ldots, a_n$ are integers, the $a_i$'s are relatively prime, and $r$ and $s$
are relatively prime.

> **Deferred:** one polynomial over two coefficient rings at once is the obstacle, and it
> blocks the three results that follow. His proof rewrites a rational-coefficient $p(x)$ with
> integer coefficients, so $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ and its $\mathbb{Z}$-coefficient sub-structure are
> both in play; `std/field/polynomial.b4m` fixes a single opaque coefficient field, with no
> embedding at the coefficient layer. A second coefficient sort with an embedding is a new
> construction rather than a missing lemma.
>
> His $d_0 \cdots d_n$ and "$d$ the greatest common divisor of $d_0, \ldots, d_n$" are *not*
> obstacles: `std/integer/product.b4m` folds a product over an indexed $\mathbb{Z}$ sequence,
> and the family gcd is `std/integer/family-gcd.b4m`'s `isFamilyGcd`. That one is carried as a
> RELATION rather than a fold, because `std/sequence.b4m`'s `combine` is a total function with
> no guard slot and a total $\gcd$ would have to invent a value at $\gcd(0,0)$ — which the
> library does not ship. Extending `std/integer/lattice.b4m`'s existential $\gcd$ along the
> index needs no such invention.

**Theorem 17.13 (Gauss's Lemma)** Let $p(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ be a monic polynomial such that
$p(x)$ factors into a product of two polynomials $\alpha(x)$ and $\beta(x)$ in
$\mathbb{Q}[x]$, where the degrees of both $\alpha(x)$ and $\beta(x)$ are less than the degree
of $p(x)$. Then $p(x) = a(x) b(x)$, where $a(x)$ and $b(x)$ are monic polynomials in
$\mathbb{Z}[x]$ with $\deg \alpha(x) = \deg a(x)$ and $\deg \beta(x) = \deg b(x)$.

> **Deferred:** rests on Lemma 17.12, and needs the same $\mathbb{Z}[x]$-inside-$\mathbb{Q}[x]$
> relationship.

**Corollary 17.14 (Rational Root Theorem)** Let
$p(x) = a_n x^n + \cdots + a_0 \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ with $a_n \neq 0$. If $r/s$ is a zero of
$p(x)$ with $\gcd(r, s) = 1$, then $r \mid a_0$ and $s \mid a_n$.

> **Deferred:** rests on Gauss's Lemma.

**Theorem 17.15 (Eisenstein's Criterion)** Let $p$ be a prime and suppose that
$$f(x) = a_n x^n + \cdots + a_0 \in \mathbb{Z}[x].$$
If $p \mid a_i$ for $i = 0, 1, \ldots, n-1$, but $p \nmid a_n$ and $p^2 \nmid a_0$, then
$f(x)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.

> **Deferred:** rests on Gauss's Lemma, and its hypothesis quantifies a divisibility condition
> over the coefficient INDEX RANGE ($p \mid a_i$ for every $i < n$), which is expressible, but
> the proof's reduction mod $p$ needs $\mathbb{Z}_p[x]$ alongside $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ — again two
> coefficient rings at once.

## Ideals in $F[x]$

**Theorem 17.16** If $F$ is a field, then every ideal in $F[x]$ is a principal ideal.

**Proof.** Let $I$ be an ideal of $F[x]$. If $I$ is the zero ideal, the theorem is easily
true. Suppose that $I$ is a nontrivial ideal in $F[x]$, and let $p(x) \in I$ be a nonzero
element of minimal degree. If $\deg p(x) = 0$, then $p(x)$ is a nonzero constant and $1$ must
be in $I$. Since $1$ generates all of $F[x]$, $\langle 1 \rangle = I = F[x]$ and $I$ is again
a principal ideal.

Now assume that $\deg p(x) \geq 1$ and let $f(x)$ be any element in $I$. By the division
algorithm there exist $q(x)$ and $r(x)$ in $F[x]$ such that $f(x) = p(x) q(x) + r(x)$ and
$\deg r(x) < \deg p(x)$. Since $f(x), p(x) \in I$ and $I$ is an ideal,
$r(x) = f(x) - p(x) q(x)$ is also in $I$. However, since we chose $p(x)$ to be of minimal
degree, $r(x)$ must be the zero polynomial. Since we can write any element $f(x)$ in $I$ as
$p(x) q(x)$ for some $q(x) \in F[x]$, it must be the case that
$I = \langle p(x) \rangle$. $\blacksquare$

The ideal is carried as a membership predicate, the idiom §16.3 uses — there is no sort of
subsets of $F[x]$. His "let $p(x) \in I$ be a nonzero element of minimal degree" is a
hypothesis rather than a construction: picking a least element of an ideal needs
well-ordering over a SET of polynomials, which is the comprehension limit §16.4 also meets.

```2b4m
// Judson's ideal of F[x]: closed under difference, absorbing on both sides.
pred inIdeal(f: Poly)
axiom idealClosedUnderSub: forall a, b: Poly;
  inIdeal(a) -> inIdeal(b) -> inIdeal(fieldpoly.addP(a, fieldpoly.negP(b)))
axiom idealAbsorbsOnTheRight: forall a, f: Poly;
  inIdeal(a) -> inIdeal(mul(a, f))
```

One piece of additive algebra is needed first: adding a term and then subtracting it
returns what was there.

```2b4m
theorem theAddedTermCancels: forall a, r: Poly;
  add(add(a, r), fieldpoly.negP(a)) = r
proof
  @generalize-a |
    fix a: Poly {
      @generalize-r |
        fix r: Poly {
          @commute-the-negation-in |
            add(add(a, r), fieldpoly.negP(a)) = add(fieldpoly.negP(a), add(a, r))
            [using specialize fieldpoly.polyAddIsCommutative(add(a, r), fieldpoly.negP(a))]
          @the-summand-is-recovered |
            r = add(fieldpoly.negP(a), add(a, r))
            [using specialize fieldpoly.aSumRecoversItsSummand(a, r)]
          @the-recovery-flipped |
            add(fieldpoly.negP(a), add(a, r)) = r
            [by symmetry the-summand-is-recovered]
          @conclusion-the-term-cancels |
            add(add(a, r), fieldpoly.negP(a)) = r
            [using chain commute-the-negation-in the-recovery-flipped]
        }
      @discharge-r |
        forall r9: Poly; add(add(a, r9), fieldpoly.negP(a)) = r9
        [by generalize generalize-r]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall a, r: Poly; add(add(a, r), fieldpoly.negP(a)) = r
    [by generalize generalize-a]
qed
```

His central step: the remainder lies in the ideal. "Since $f(x), p(x) \in I$ and $I$ is an
ideal, $r(x) = f(x) - p(x) q(x)$ is also in $I$."

```2b4m
theorem theRemainderLiesInTheIdeal: forall f, p, q: Poly;
  inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p) ->
  inIdeal(add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q))))
proof
  @generalize-f |
    fix f: Poly {
      @generalize-p |
        fix p: Poly {
          @generalize-q |
            fix q: Poly {
              @given-f-in-the-ideal |
                assume inIdeal(f) {
                  @given-p-in-the-ideal |
                    assume inIdeal(p) {
                      @f-in-the-ideal |
                        inIdeal(f)
                        [by hypothesis given-f-in-the-ideal]
                      @p-in-the-ideal |
                        inIdeal(p)
                        [by hypothesis given-p-in-the-ideal]
                      // the ideal absorbs p(x)q(x)
                      @the-multiple-is-in-the-ideal |
                        inIdeal(mul(p, q))
                        [using specialize idealAbsorbsOnTheRight(p, q) p-in-the-ideal]
                      // and is closed under the difference
                      @conclusion-the-remainder-is-in-the-ideal |
                        inIdeal(add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q))))
                        [using specialize idealClosedUnderSub(f, mul(p, q)) f-in-the-ideal the-multiple-is-in-the-ideal]
                    }
                  @p-in-implies-the-remainder-in |
                    inIdeal(p) -> inIdeal(add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q))))
                    [by discharge given-p-in-the-ideal]
                }
              @both-in-imply-the-remainder-in |
                inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p) -> inIdeal(add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q))))
                [by discharge given-f-in-the-ideal]
            }
          @discharge-q |
            forall q9: Poly;
              inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p) -> inIdeal(add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q9))))
            [by generalize generalize-q]
        }
      @discharge-p |
        forall p9, q9: Poly;
          inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p9) -> inIdeal(add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p9, q9))))
        [by generalize generalize-p]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall f, p, q: Poly;
      inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p) -> inIdeal(add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q))))
    [by generalize generalize-f]
qed
```

And his conclusion from minimality: "since we chose $p(x)$ to be of minimal degree, $r(x)$
must be the zero polynomial." Minimality is the hypothesis — every nonzero member of $I$ has
degree at least $\deg p$ — so a remainder of strictly lower degree cannot be a nonzero member.

```2b4m
theorem minimalityForcesTheRemainderToVanish: forall p, r: Poly;
  (forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
  (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p)))) ->
  inIdeal(r) -> less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) ->
  r = ZEROP
proof
  @generalize-p |
    fix p: Poly {
      @generalize-r |
        fix r: Poly {
          @given-minimality |
            assume forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
              (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p))) {
              @given-r-in-the-ideal |
                assume inIdeal(r) {
                  @given-r-is-lower |
                    assume less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) {
                      @minimality |
                        forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
                          (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p)))
                        [by hypothesis given-minimality]
                      @r-in-the-ideal |
                        inIdeal(r)
                        [by hypothesis given-r-in-the-ideal]
                      @r-is-lower |
                        less_than(degree(r), degree(p))
                        [by hypothesis given-r-is-lower]
                      // were r nonzero, minimality would forbid its lower degree
                      @minimality-at-r |
                        inIdeal(r) -> (not (r = ZEROP)) ->
                          (not less_than(degree(r), degree(p)))
                        [using specialize minimality(r)]
                      @given-r-nonzero |
                        assume not (r = ZEROP) {
                          @r-nonzero |
                            not (r = ZEROP)
                            [by hypothesis given-r-nonzero]
                          @r-is-not-lower |
                            not less_than(degree(r), degree(p))
                            [using tautology minimality-at-r r-in-the-ideal r-nonzero]
                          @contradiction |
                            r = ZEROP
                            [by ex_falso r-is-lower r-is-not-lower]
                        }
                      @nonzero-is-absurd |
                        (not (r = ZEROP)) -> r = ZEROP
                        [by discharge given-r-nonzero]
                      @conclusion-the-remainder-vanishes |
                        r = ZEROP
                        [using tautology nonzero-is-absurd]
                    }
                  @lower-implies-zero |
                    less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) -> r = ZEROP
                    [by discharge given-r-is-lower]
                }
              @in-the-ideal-implies |
                inIdeal(r) -> less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) -> r = ZEROP
                [by discharge given-r-in-the-ideal]
            }
          @minimality-implies |
            (forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
              (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p)))) ->
              inIdeal(r) -> less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) -> r = ZEROP
            [by discharge given-minimality]
        }
      @discharge-r |
        forall r9: Poly;
          (forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
          (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p)))) ->
          inIdeal(r9) -> less_than(degree(r9), degree(p)) -> r9 = ZEROP
        [by generalize generalize-r]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall p, r: Poly;
      (forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
      (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p)))) ->
      inIdeal(r) -> less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) -> r = ZEROP
    [by generalize generalize-p]
qed
```

Putting the two together is his "since we can write any element $f(x)$ in $I$ as
$p(x) q(x)$": given the division and the minimality hypothesis, every member of the ideal is a
multiple of $p$.

```2b4m
theorem everyIdealMemberIsAMultiple: forall f, p, q, r: Poly;
  (forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
  (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p)))) ->
  inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p) ->
  f = add(mul(p, q), r) ->
  less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) ->
  f = mul(p, q)
proof
  @generalize-f |
    fix f: Poly {
      @generalize-p |
        fix p: Poly {
          @generalize-q |
            fix q: Poly {
              @generalize-r |
                fix r: Poly {
                  @given-minimality |
                    assume forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
                      (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p))) {
                      @given-f-in |
                        assume inIdeal(f) {
                          @given-p-in |
                            assume inIdeal(p) {
                              @given-the-division |
                                assume f = add(mul(p, q), r) {
                                  @given-r-is-lower |
                                    assume less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) {
                                      @minimality |
                                        forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
                                          (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p)))
                                        [by hypothesis given-minimality]
                                      @f-in |
                                        inIdeal(f)
                                        [by hypothesis given-f-in]
                                      @p-in |
                                        inIdeal(p)
                                        [by hypothesis given-p-in]
                                      @the-division |
                                        f = add(mul(p, q), r)
                                        [by hypothesis given-the-division]
                                      @r-is-lower |
                                        less_than(degree(r), degree(p))
                                        [by hypothesis given-r-is-lower]
                                      @reflexivity-of-the-difference |
                                        add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q))) =
                                          add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q)))
                                        [by reflexivity]

                                      // r = f - pq lies in the ideal...

                                        inIdeal(add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q))))

                                      // ...and that difference IS r, by the division

                                        add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q))) =
                                          add(add(mul(p, q), r), fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q)))
                                        [by rewrite the-division reflexivity-of-the-difference]
                                      @the-cancellation |
                                        add(add(mul(p, q), r), fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q))) = r
                                        [using specialize theAddedTermCancels(mul(p, q), r)]
                                      @the-difference-is-r |
                                        add(f, fieldpoly.negP(mul(p, q))) = r
                                        [using chain the-difference-expanded the-cancellation]
                                      @r-is-in-the-ideal |
                                        inIdeal(r)
                                        [by rewrite the-difference-is-r the-difference-is-in-the-ideal]
                                      // so minimality forces it to vanish
                                      @r-vanishes |
                                        r = ZEROP
                                        [using specialize minimalityForcesTheRemainderToVanish(p, r) minimality r-is-in-the-ideal r-is-lower]
                                      @f-is-the-multiple-plus-zero |
                                        f = add(mul(p, q), ZEROP)
                                        [by rewrite r-vanishes the-division]
                                      @adding-zero |
                                        add(mul(p, q), ZEROP) = mul(p, q)
                                        [using specialize fieldpoly.polyAddZeroRight(mul(p, q))]
                                      @conclusion-f-is-a-multiple |
                                        f = mul(p, q)
                                        [using chain f-is-the-multiple-plus-zero adding-zero]
                                    }
                                  @lower-degree-implies-the-multiple |
                                    less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) -> f = mul(p, q)
                                    [by discharge given-r-is-lower]
                                }
                              @the-division-implies-the-multiple |
                                f = add(mul(p, q), r) ->
                                  less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) -> f = mul(p, q)
                                [by discharge given-the-division]
                            }
                          @p-in-the-ideal-implies-the-multiple |
                            inIdeal(p) -> f = add(mul(p, q), r) ->
                              less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) -> f = mul(p, q)
                            [by discharge given-p-in]
                        }
                      @both-in-the-ideal-imply-the-multiple |
                        inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p) -> f = add(mul(p, q), r) ->
                          less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) -> f = mul(p, q)
                        [by discharge given-f-in]
                    }
                  @minimality-implies-every-member-is-a-multiple |
                    (forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
                      (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p)))) ->
                      inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p) -> f = add(mul(p, q), r) ->
                      less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) -> f = mul(p, q)
                    [by discharge given-minimality]
                }
              @discharge-r |
                forall r9: Poly;
                  (forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
                  (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p)))) ->
                  inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p) -> f = add(mul(p, q), r9) ->
                  less_than(degree(r9), degree(p)) -> f = mul(p, q)
                [by generalize generalize-r]
            }
          @discharge-q |
            forall q9, r9: Poly;
              (forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
              (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p)))) ->
              inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p) -> f = add(mul(p, q9), r9) ->
              less_than(degree(r9), degree(p)) -> f = mul(p, q9)
            [by generalize generalize-q]
        }
      @discharge-p |
        forall p9, q9, r9: Poly;
          (forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
          (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p9)))) ->
          inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p9) -> f = add(mul(p9, q9), r9) ->
          less_than(degree(r9), degree(p9)) -> f = mul(p9, q9)
        [by generalize generalize-p]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall f, p, q, r: Poly;
      (forall x: Poly; inIdeal(x) -> (not (x = ZEROP)) ->
      (not less_than(degree(x), degree(p)))) ->
      inIdeal(f) -> inIdeal(p) -> f = add(mul(p, q), r) ->
      less_than(degree(r), degree(p)) -> f = mul(p, q)
    [by generalize generalize-f]
```

**Theorem 17.17** Let $F$ be a field and suppose that $p(x) \in F[x]$. Then the ideal
generated by $p(x)$ is maximal if and only if $p(x)$ is irreducible.

**Proof.** Suppose that $p(x)$ generates a maximal ideal of $F[x]$. Then
$\langle p(x) \rangle$ is also a prime ideal of $F[x]$. Since a maximal ideal must be
properly contained inside $F[x]$, $p(x)$ cannot be a constant polynomial. Let us assume that
$p(x)$ factors into two polynomials of lesser degree, say $p(x) = f(x) g(x)$. Since
$\langle p(x) \rangle$ is a prime ideal one of these factors, say $f(x)$, is in
$\langle p(x) \rangle$ and therefore be a multiple of $p(x)$. But this would imply that
$\langle p(x) \rangle \subset \langle f(x) \rangle$, which is impossible since
$\langle p(x) \rangle$ is maximal.

Conversely, suppose that $p(x)$ is irreducible over $F[x]$. Let $I$ be an ideal in $F[x]$
containing $\langle p(x) \rangle$. By Theorem 17.16, $I$ is a principal ideal; hence,
$I = \langle f(x) \rangle$ for some $f(x) \in F[x]$. Since $p(x) \in I$, it must be the case
that $p(x) = f(x) g(x)$ for some $g(x) \in F[x]$. However, $p(x)$ is irreducible; hence,
either $f(x)$ or $g(x)$ is a constant polynomial. If $f(x)$ is constant, then $I = F[x]$ and
we are done. If $g(x)$ is constant, then $f(x)$ is a constant multiple of $I$ and
$I = \langle p(x) \rangle$. Thus, there are no proper ideals of $F[x]$ that properly contain
$\langle p(x)\rangle$. $\blacksquare$

> **Ideals are membership predicates and primality is a hypothesis.** An ideal here is a
> `pred`, not a value (the §16.3 idiom), so "$I = \langle f \rangle$" reads as "every member
> of $I$ is a multiple of $f$" and "$I = F[x]$" as "$f$ divides every polynomial". His
> "$\langle p(x) \rangle$ is also a prime ideal" cites §16.4 rather than reproving it, and
> §16.4's route to that result runs through the factor ring $R/I$ over `ring.Ring` — a sort
> $F[x]$ is not, and one a model cannot reach, since `std/ring/factor.b4m` declares `Quot`
> and `cls` over `Ring` directly. So primality's *consequence* is what travels: a factor of
> strictly smaller degree is never a multiple of $p$, which is the impossibility his
> containment argument names.

```2b4m
import polyideal <<< "std/field/ideal.b4m"

// forward: his assumed factorisation cannot stand, because a lower-degree factor is never
// a multiple of p — deg(p·h) = deg p + deg h never sits below deg p
theorem aLowerDegreeFactorIsNotAMultiple: forall p, f, h: Poly;
  (not (p = ZEROP)) -> (not (h = ZEROP)) ->
  less_than(degree(f), degree(p)) ->
  (not (f = mul(p, h)))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall p, f, h: Poly;
      (not (p = ZEROP)) -> (not (h = ZEROP)) ->
      less_than(degree(f), degree(p)) ->
      (not (f = mul(p, h)))
    [using import(polyideal) aLowerDegreeFactorIsNotAMultiple]
qed

// converse: the generator of an ideal containing an irreducible either divides everything
// (so the ideal is all of F[x]) or is itself a multiple of p (so the ideal is <p> again)
theorem anIrreducibleGeneratorAdmitsNoProperExtension: forall p, f, g: Poly;
  (not (f = ZEROP)) -> (not (g = ZEROP)) ->
  isIrreducible(p) ->
  p = mul(f, g) ->
  ((forall h: Poly; exists w: Poly; h = mul(w, f)) or
  (exists v: Poly; f = mul(v, p)))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall p, f, g: Poly;
      (not (f = ZEROP)) -> (not (g = ZEROP)) ->
      isIrreducible(p) ->
      p = mul(f, g) ->
      ((forall h: Poly; exists w: Poly; h = mul(w, f)) or
      (exists v: Poly; f = mul(v, p)))
    [using import(polyideal) anIrreducibleGeneratorAdmitsNoProperExtension]
qed
```
