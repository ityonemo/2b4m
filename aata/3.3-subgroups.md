# Subgroups

A literate `2b4m` transliteration of **Chapter 3 ("Groups"), §3.3 "Subgroups"** of
Thomas W. Judson's *Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications* (source:
`vendor/aata/src/groups.xml`, lines 923–1202; © 1997–2026 Judson, GFDL 1.3). The
book's prose is reproduced in order — the definition of a subgroup, its examples,
the subgroup criterion, and the one-step test — with a machine-checked `2b4m` proof
following each result. Run `2b4m check aata/3.3-subgroups.md` to verify.

The subgroup theory lives in `std/subgroup.b4m`, layered on `std/group.b4m`. A
subgroup is modeled as a **membership predicate** `inSubgroup(g)` over the group's
underlying sort, characterized by the three subgroup-criterion conditions; the results
below cite that library, aliased to the book's notation.

## Definition

**Definition.** Let $G$ be a group and $H$ a subset of $G$. Then $H$ is a
**subgroup** of $G$ if $H$ is itself a group under the operation induced by $G$.
We write $H \leq G$; if $H$ is a proper subset of $G$, then $H$ is a **proper
subgroup**, written $H \lt G$. The subgroup $\{e\}$ consisting of the identity
alone is the **trivial subgroup**; every group $G$ has $\{e\}$ and $G$ itself as
subgroups.

We import the subgroup theory and alias it to the book's notation — the group $G$
and the subgroup-membership relation $H$ (writing `inH(g)` for "$g \in H$").

```2b4m
import subgroup <<< "std/subgroup.b4m"

// the book's notation: the ambient group's underlying sort, its identity/op/inverse, and
// the subgroup as a membership predicate (`inH(g)` reads "g ∈ H").
sort G = subgroup.Grp
const e = subgroup.E
func op = subgroup.op
func inv = subgroup.inverse
pred inH = subgroup.inSubgroup
fact subgroupHasIdentity = subgroup.subgroupHasIdentity
fact subgroupClosedOp = subgroup.subgroupClosedOp
fact subgroupClosedInverse = subgroup.subgroupClosedInverse
fact subgroup2HasIdentity = subgroup.subgroup2HasIdentity
fact subgroup2ClosedOp = subgroup.subgroup2ClosedOp
fact subgroup2ClosedInverse = subgroup.subgroup2ClosedInverse
fact grpAssoc = subgroup.grpAssoc
fact grpIdentityLeft = subgroup.grpIdentityLeft
fact grpIdentityRight = subgroup.grpIdentityRight
fact grpInverseLeft = subgroup.grpInverseLeft
fact grpInverseRight = subgroup.grpInverseRight
fact inverseUnique = subgroup.inverseUnique
fact invInvolution = subgroup.invInvolution
fact invProduct = subgroup.invProduct
```

> *`std/subgroup.b4m` characterizes $H$ by the three subgroup-criterion conditions
> as axioms — `subgroupHasIdentity` ($e \in H$), `subgroupClosedOp` (closed under
> the operation), `subgroupClosedInverse` (closed under inverses). This is the
> "iff" criterion below taken as the working definition of a subgroup; the results
> derive from it exactly as the book derives them from the definition.*

## Examples

The book gives several concrete subgroups; each needs an underlying sort `2b4m` does not
model (rationals, complex numbers, matrices, finite/product groups), so they are
summarized here rather than formalized — as `3.2-groups.md` handles the §3.1
examples.

- $\mathbb{Q}^* \leq \mathbb{R}^*$ — the nonzero rationals under multiplication
  sit inside the nonzero reals (needs a quotient/pair sort).
- $H = \{1, -1, i, -i\} \leq \mathbb{C}^*$ — the fourth roots of unity (complex
  sort).
- $SL_2(\mathbb{R}) \leq GL_2(\mathbb{R})$ — the special linear group, matrices of
  determinant $1$ (2×2 matrix sort).
- $\{0, 2\} \leq \mathbb{Z}_4$, and the three order-2 subgroups of
  $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ (finite / product sorts).

> *A cautionary non-example (groups.xml:1025): $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$ is a group under
> multiplication and a subset of $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ (a group under addition), yet
> is not a subgroup of it — the operations differ ($I + (-I) = 0 \notin
> GL_2(\mathbb{R})$). A subgroup must use the SAME operation, which is exactly why
> `std/subgroup.b4m` shares the ambient group's `op`/`inverse`.*

## The one-step subgroup test

The book proves two characterizations. The first (groups.xml:1103) is the
three-condition **subgroup criterion** — $H \leq G$ iff $e \in H$, $H$ is closed
under products, and $H$ is closed under inverses — which `std/subgroup.b4m` takes
as its axioms. The second consolidates all three into a single test.

**Proposition (One-step subgroup test).** Let $H$ be a subset of a group $G$. Then
$H$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if $H \neq \emptyset$ and, whenever $g, h \in
H$, the element $g h^{-1}$ is in $H$.

The **forward** direction: a subgroup is closed under $g h^{-1}$. Since $h \in H$,
its inverse $h^{-1} \in H$ (closure under inverses); then by closure under the
operation, $g h^{-1} \in H$. This is `subgroupClosedDivide` in the library.

The subgroup's underlying sort is written `H` — a **predicated sort** `G where inH`, so
`forall g, h: H; …` reads "for subgroup elements $g, h$" and the membership
hypotheses stay implicit.

```2b4m
// the subgroup's underlying sort as a predicated sort (G restricted to members).
sort H = G where inH

// forward: H ≤ G ⟹ H is closed under g·h⁻¹.
theorem closedUnderDivide: forall g, h: H; inH(op(g, inv(h)))
proof
  @generalize-g |
    fix g: H {
      @g-is-a-member |
        inH(g)
        [by predicate generalize-g]
      @generalize-h |
        fix h: H {
          @h-is-a-member |
            inH(h)
            [by predicate generalize-h]
          @inv-of-h-in-H |
            inH(inv(h))
            [using specialize subgroupClosedInverse(h) h-is-a-member]
          @divide-is-a-member |
            inH(op(g, inv(h)))
            [using specialize subgroupClosedOp(g, inv(h)) g-is-a-member inv-of-h-in-H]
        }
      @discharge-h |
        forall h: H; inH(op(g, inv(h)))
        [by generalize generalize-h]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall g, h: H; inH(op(g, inv(h)))
    [by generalize generalize-g]
qed
```

The **converse** — a nonempty subset closed under $g h^{-1}$ satisfies the three
conditions — is the substantive half. Because `2b4m` cannot quantify over subsets,
`std/subgroup.b4m` expresses "an arbitrary such subset" as a second predicate
`oneStepSubset` with exactly the two hypotheses ($H \neq \emptyset$ and closure
under $g h^{-1}$), and derives each subgroup condition. The book's three key
computations transfer verbatim:

- **identity:** for any member $g$, $g g^{-1} = e \in H$;
- **inverses:** $e\, g^{-1} = g^{-1} \in H$;
- **products:** $h_1 (h_2^{-1})^{-1} = h_1 h_2 \in H$ (using $(h_2^{-1})^{-1} =
  h_2$).

We alias that second subset to the book's notation and cite the three derived
conditions.

```2b4m
// the book's "nonempty, closed under g·h⁻¹" subset K, and the three subgroup
// conditions the converse establishes for it.
pred inK = subgroup.oneStepSubset
fact oneStepInhabited = subgroup.oneStepInhabited
fact oneStepClosedDivide = subgroup.oneStepClosedDivide

theorem oneStepGivesIdentity: inK(e)
proof
  @some-member-exists |
    exists g: G; inK(g)
    [by cite oneStepInhabited]
  @with-member |
    unpack g: G from some-member-exists {
      @member-in-subset |
        inK(g)
        [by hypothesis with-member]
      @self-divide-in-subset |
        inK(op(g, inv(g)))
        [using specialize oneStepClosedDivide(g, g) member-in-subset member-in-subset]
      @self-divide-is-identity |
        op(g, inv(g)) = e
        [using specialize grpInverseRight(g)]
      @conclusion-identity-in-subset |
        inK(e)
        [by rewrite self-divide-is-identity self-divide-in-subset]
    }
  @conclusion |
    inK(e)
    [by unpacked with-member]
qed

theorem oneStepGivesInverses: forall g: G; inK(g) -> inK(inv(g))
proof
  @generalize-g |
    fix g: G {
      @given-g-in-subset |
        assume inK(g) {
          @g-in-subset |
            inK(g)
            [by hypothesis given-g-in-subset]
          @identity-in-subset |
            inK(e)
            [by cite oneStepGivesIdentity]
          @identity-divide-g-in-subset |
            inK(op(e, inv(g)))
            [using specialize oneStepClosedDivide(e, g) identity-in-subset g-in-subset]
          @identity-divide-g-is-inv |
            op(e, inv(g)) = inv(g)
            [using specialize grpIdentityLeft(inv(g))]
          @conclusion-inv-in-subset |
            inK(inv(g))
            [by rewrite identity-divide-g-is-inv identity-divide-g-in-subset]
        }
      @g-in-subset-implies-inv |
        inK(g) -> inK(inv(g))
        [by discharge given-g-in-subset]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall g: G; inK(g) -> inK(inv(g))
    [by generalize generalize-g]
qed

theorem oneStepGivesProducts: forall h1, h2: G; inK(h1) -> inK(h2) -> inK(op(h1, h2))
proof
  @generalize-h1 |
    fix h1: G {
      @generalize-h2 |
        fix h2: G {
          @given-h1-in-subset |
            assume inK(h1) {
              @given-h2-in-subset |
                assume inK(h2) {
                  @h1-in-subset |
                    inK(h1)
                    [by hypothesis given-h1-in-subset]
                  @h2-in-subset |
                    inK(h2)
                    [by hypothesis given-h2-in-subset]
                  @inv-h2-in-subset |
                    inK(inv(h2))
                    [using specialize oneStepGivesInverses(h2) h2-in-subset]
                  @h1-divide-inv-h2-in-subset |
                    inK(op(h1, inv(inv(h2))))
                    [using specialize oneStepClosedDivide(h1, inv(h2)) h1-in-subset inv-h2-in-subset]
                  @double-inv-h2 |
                    inv(inv(h2)) = h2
                    [using specialize invInvolution(h2)]
                  @conclusion-product-in-subset |
                    inK(op(h1, h2))
                    [by rewrite double-inv-h2 h1-divide-inv-h2-in-subset]
                }
              @h2-in-subset-implies-product |
                inK(h2) -> inK(op(h1, h2))
                [by discharge given-h2-in-subset]
            }
          @h1-in-subset-implies-rest |
            inK(h1) -> inK(h2) -> inK(op(h1, h2))
            [by discharge given-h1-in-subset]
        }
      @discharge-h2 |
        forall h2: G; inK(h1) -> inK(h2) -> inK(op(h1, h2))
        [by generalize generalize-h2]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall h1, h2: G; inK(h1) -> inK(h2) -> inK(op(h1, h2))
    [by generalize generalize-h1]
qed
```

Having the identity, inverses, and products, $K$ meets the subgroup criterion —
so $K \leq G$, completing the test.

## The intersection of subgroups

A natural consequence (developed in the §3.3 exercises, groups.xml:1938): the
**intersection of two subgroups is a subgroup**. If $H$ and $K$ are subgroups,
$H \cap K$ contains $e$ (both do), and is closed under products and inverses
(apply each closure in $H$ and in $K$). `std/subgroup.b4m` models the second
subgroup as `inSubgroup2` and proves the three conditions for the conjunction
$g \in H \wedge g \in K$; we alias it and cite them.

```2b4m
// a second subgroup K, and membership in the intersection H ∩ K.
pred inK2 = subgroup.inSubgroup2

theorem intersectionHasIdentity: inH(e) and inK2(e)
proof
  @identity-in-first |
    inH(e)
    [by cite subgroupHasIdentity]
  @identity-in-second |
    inK2(e)
    [by cite subgroup2HasIdentity]
  @conclusion |
    inH(e) and inK2(e)
    [by both identity-in-first identity-in-second]
qed

theorem intersectionClosedUnderOp: forall a, b: G; (inH(a) and inK2(a)) -> (inH(b) and inK2(b)) -> (inH(op(a, b)) and inK2(op(a, b)))
proof
  @generalize-a |
    fix a: G {
      @generalize-b |
        fix b: G {
          @given-a-in-both |
            assume inH(a) and inK2(a) {
              @given-b-in-both |
                assume inH(b) and inK2(b) {
                  @a-in-both |
                    inH(a) and inK2(a)
                    [by hypothesis given-a-in-both]
                  @b-in-both |
                    inH(b) and inK2(b)
                    [by hypothesis given-b-in-both]
                  @a-in-first |
                    inH(a)
                    [by and_lhs a-in-both]
                  @a-in-second |
                    inK2(a)
                    [by and_rhs a-in-both]
                  @b-in-first |
                    inH(b)
                    [by and_lhs b-in-both]
                  @b-in-second |
                    inK2(b)
                    [by and_rhs b-in-both]
                  @product-in-first |
                    inH(op(a, b))
                    [using specialize subgroupClosedOp(a, b) a-in-first b-in-first]
                  @product-in-second |
                    inK2(op(a, b))
                    [using specialize subgroup2ClosedOp(a, b) a-in-second b-in-second]
                  @conclusion-product-in-both |
                    inH(op(a, b)) and inK2(op(a, b))
                    [by both product-in-first product-in-second]
                }
              @b-in-both-implies-product |
                (inH(b) and inK2(b)) -> (inH(op(a, b)) and inK2(op(a, b)))
                [by discharge given-b-in-both]
            }
          @a-in-both-implies-rest |
            (inH(a) and inK2(a)) -> (inH(b) and inK2(b)) -> (inH(op(a, b)) and inK2(op(a, b)))
            [by discharge given-a-in-both]
        }
      @discharge-b |
        forall b: G; (inH(a) and inK2(a)) -> (inH(b) and inK2(b)) -> (inH(op(a, b)) and inK2(op(a, b)))
        [by generalize generalize-b]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall a, b: G; (inH(a) and inK2(a)) -> (inH(b) and inK2(b)) -> (inH(op(a, b)) and inK2(op(a, b)))
    [by generalize generalize-a]
qed

theorem intersectionClosedUnderInverse: forall a: G; (inH(a) and inK2(a)) -> (inH(inv(a)) and inK2(inv(a)))
proof
  @generalize-a |
    fix a: G {
      @given-a-in-both |
        assume inH(a) and inK2(a) {
          @a-in-both |
            inH(a) and inK2(a)
            [by hypothesis given-a-in-both]
          @a-in-first |
            inH(a)
            [by and_lhs a-in-both]
          @a-in-second |
            inK2(a)
            [by and_rhs a-in-both]
          @inv-in-first |
            inH(inv(a))
            [using specialize subgroupClosedInverse(a) a-in-first]
          @inv-in-second |
            inK2(inv(a))
            [using specialize subgroup2ClosedInverse(a) a-in-second]
          @conclusion-inv-in-both |
            inH(inv(a)) and inK2(inv(a))
            [by both inv-in-first inv-in-second]
        }
      @a-in-both-implies-inv |
        (inH(a) and inK2(a)) -> (inH(inv(a)) and inK2(inv(a)))
        [by discharge given-a-in-both]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall a: G; (inH(a) and inK2(a)) -> (inH(inv(a)) and inK2(inv(a)))
    [by generalize generalize-a]
qed
```

The intersection contains the identity and is closed under both the operation and
inverses — the three conditions — so $H \cap K \leq G$.
