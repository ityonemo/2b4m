# The Isomorphism Theorems

A literate `2b4m` transliteration of **Chapter 11 ("Homomorphisms"), §11.2 "The
Isomorphism Theorems"** of Thomas W. Judson's *Abstract Algebra: Theory and
Applications* (source: `vendor/aata/src/homomorph.xml`, lines 285–566; © 1997–2026
Judson, GFDL 1.3). Run `2b4m check aata/11.2-the-isomorphism-theorems.md` to verify.

## Factor groups and homomorphic images

Judson opens: "Although it is not evident at first, factor groups correspond exactly to
homomorphic images, and we can use factor groups to study homomorphisms. We already
know that with every group homomorphism $\phi : G \rightarrow H$ we can associate a
normal subgroup of $G$, $\ker \phi$. The converse is also true; that is, every normal
subgroup of a group $G$ gives rise to a homomorphism of groups."

**Definition.** Let $H$ be a normal subgroup of $G$. Define the **natural** or
**canonical homomorphism** $\phi : G \rightarrow G/H$ by $\phi(g) = gH$. This is indeed
a homomorphism, since
$$\phi(g_1 g_2) = g_1 g_2 H = g_1 H g_2 H = \phi(g_1)\phi(g_2).$$
The kernel of this homomorphism is $H$.

$G/H$ exists as a **sort** — this is what §10.1 recorded as missing, and
`std/group/factor.b4m` now supplies it on `std/integer/mod-n.b4m`'s quotient pattern: a
fresh sort with a class map onto it, a surjectivity axiom so representatives can be
unpacked, and the congruence $\mathrm{cls}(a) = \mathrm{cls}(b) \iff a^{-1}b \in N$.
The class map **is** Judson's canonical homomorphism, and both halves of his claim are
theorems.

```2b4m
import group_factor <<< "std/group/factor.b4m"

sort G = group_factor.G
sort Quot = group_factor.Quot
const EG = group_factor.EG
const EQ = group_factor.EQ
func opG = group_factor.opG
func invG = group_factor.invG
func opQ = group_factor.opQ
func invQ = group_factor.invQ
func cls = group_factor.cls
pred inN = group_factor.inN

// the canonical homomorphism, and its kernel
theorem theCanonicalMapIsAHomomorphism: forall a: G; forall b: G;
  cls(opG(a, b)) = opQ(cls(a), cls(b))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall a: G; forall b: G; cls(opG(a, b)) = opQ(cls(a), cls(b))
    [using import(group_factor) clsPreservesOp]
qed

theorem theCanonicalMapsKernelIsN: forall g: G; cls(g) = EQ iff inN(g)
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall g: G; cls(g) = EQ iff inN(g)
    [using import(group_factor) clsKernelIsN]
qed
```

## The one limit this section runs into, stated once

Every theorem below concludes with a $\cong$. That relation **cannot be named**: a group
here is a *theory* — a sort plus operations plus axioms — not a value inhabiting some
sort of groups, so "for all groups $G$, $H$ …" is out of reach. §9.1 met the same wall
for Theorem 9.10 and settled how it goes: **every instance is provable; only the
quantification is not.** See `CHAPTER9-PAINPOINTS`.

So each theorem below is written as "here are the declared groups, here is the map, and
here are its properties" — the mathematical content complete, the one-line $\cong$
restatement absent. This is one limit appearing four times, not four limits.

## First Isomorphism Theorem

**Theorem 11.10 (First Isomorphism Theorem).** If $\psi : G \rightarrow H$ is a group
homomorphism with $K = \ker \psi$, then $K$ is normal in $G$. Let
$\phi : G \rightarrow G/K$ be the canonical homomorphism. Then there exists a unique
isomorphism $\eta : G/K \rightarrow \psi(G)$ such that $\psi = \eta\phi$.

*Proof (Judson).* Define $\eta : G/K \rightarrow \psi(G)$ by $\eta(gK) = \psi(g)$. We
first show that $\eta$ is a well-defined map. If $g_1K = g_2K$, then for some $k \in K$,
$g_1k = g_2$; consequently,
$\eta(g_1K) = \psi(g_1) = \psi(g_1)\psi(k) = \psi(g_1k) = \psi(g_2) = \eta(g_2K)$.
We must also show that $\eta$ is a homomorphism:
$\eta(g_1Kg_2K) = \eta(g_1g_2K) = \psi(g_1g_2) = \psi(g_1)\psi(g_2) =
\eta(g_1K)\eta(g_2K)$. Clearly $\eta$ is onto $\psi(G)$. To show $\eta$ is one-to-one,
suppose $\eta(g_1K) = \eta(g_2K)$. Then $\psi(g_1) = \psi(g_2)$, so
$\psi(g_1^{-1}g_2) = e$, so $g_1^{-1}g_2$ is in the kernel; hence $g_1K = g_2K$.
$\blacksquare$

**Well-definedness is where the quotient sort earns its place.** Judson's premise
"$g_1K = g_2K$" is, with cosets as predicates, extensional agreement of two
predicates — which `rewrite` cannot consume. With the quotient as a sort it is an
*equation*, $\mathrm{cls}(a) = \mathrm{cls}(b)$, and the step is three rewrites.

```2b4m
import first_iso <<< "std/group/first-isomorphism.b4m"

sort HH = first_iso.HH
func psi = first_iso.psi
func eta = first_iso.eta
func opHH = first_iso.opHH

theorem etaIsWellDefined: forall a: G; forall b: G;
  cls(a) = cls(b) -> psi(a) = psi(b)
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall a: G; forall b: G; cls(a) = cls(b) -> psi(a) = psi(b)
    [using import(first_iso) etaIsWellDefined]
qed

theorem etaPreservesOp: forall x: Quot; forall y: Quot;
  eta(opQ(x, y)) = opHH(eta(x), eta(y))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall x: Quot; forall y: Quot; eta(opQ(x, y)) = opHH(eta(x), eta(y))
    [using import(first_iso) etaPreservesOp]
qed

theorem etaIsInjective: forall x: Quot; forall y: Quot; eta(x) = eta(y) -> x = y
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall x: Quot; forall y: Quot; eta(x) = eta(y) -> x = y
    [using import(first_iso) etaIsInjective]
qed
```

Surjectivity onto $\psi(G)$ is immediate — every element of the image is $\psi(g) =
\eta(gK)$ by construction — and `homomorphism.inImage` is where that predicate lives.
What is absent is only "there exists a **unique** isomorphism $\eta$": $\eta$ is
*declared* and its properties proved, which is the instance.

## Second Isomorphism Theorem

**Theorem 11.12 (Second Isomorphism Theorem).** Let $H$ be a subgroup of a group $G$
(not necessarily normal in $G$) and $N$ a normal subgroup of $G$. Then $HN$ is a
subgroup of $G$, $H \cap N$ is a normal subgroup of $H$, and
$$H/H \cap N \cong HN/N.$$

*Proof (Judson).* "We will first show that $HN = \{hn : h \in H, n \in N\}$ is a
subgroup of $G$. Suppose that $h_1n_1, h_2n_2 \in HN$. Since $N$ is normal,
$(h_2)^{-1}n_1h_2 \in N$. So $(h_1n_1)(h_2n_2) = h_1h_2((h_2)^{-1}n_1h_2)n_2$ is in
$HN$. The inverse of $hn \in HN$ is in $HN$ since
$(hn)^{-1} = n^{-1}h^{-1} = h^{-1}(hn^{-1}h^{-1})$. Next, we prove that $H \cap N$ is
normal in $H$. Let $h \in H$ and $n \in H \cap N$. Then $h^{-1}nh \in H$ since each
element is in $H$. Also, $h^{-1}nh \in N$ since $N$ is normal in $G$." $\blacksquare$

**Both of Judson's regroupings cost more than they read.** Each *inserts a cancelling
pair* — $h_2h_2^{-1}$ in the product, $h^{-1}h$ in the inverse — so the two sides are
**not** equal by associativity alone, and `assoc` (which right-nests both sides and
compares) cannot reach them. The cancellation is written out in each case.

A note on orientation: `std` states normality as $n \in N \Rightarrow gng^{-1} \in N$,
and Judson writes $h_2^{-1}n_1h_2$ — that is the axiom at $g = h_2^{-1}$, giving
$h_2^{-1}n_1(h_2^{-1})^{-1}$, so an involution rewrite is needed to reach his form. The
inverse half needs no flip.

```2b4m
import subgroup_product <<< "std/subgroup/product.b4m"

sort G2 = subgroup_product.G
func opG2 = subgroup_product.opG
func invG2 = subgroup_product.invG
const EG2 = subgroup_product.EG
pred inH = subgroup_product.inH
pred inNormal = subgroup_product.inN
define inProduct(x) = subgroup_product.inProduct(x)

// HN is a subgroup of G
theorem theProductHasTheIdentity: inProduct(EG2)
proof
  @conclusion |
    inProduct(EG2)
    [using import(subgroup_product) theProductHasTheIdentity]
qed

theorem theProductIsClosedUnderTheOperation: forall x: G2; forall y: G2;
  inProduct(x) -> inProduct(y) -> inProduct(opG2(x, y))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall x: G2; forall y: G2; inProduct(x) -> inProduct(y) -> inProduct(opG2(x, y))
    [using import(subgroup_product) theProductIsClosedUnderTheOperation]
qed

theorem theProductIsClosedUnderInverses: forall x: G2;
  inProduct(x) -> inProduct(invG2(x))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall x: G2; inProduct(x) -> inProduct(invG2(x))
    [using import(subgroup_product) theProductIsClosedUnderInverses]
qed

// H n N is normal in H. (That it is a SUBGROUP was already in std — the conjunction
// IS the intersection, and subgroup.b4m proves its three criteria.)
theorem theIntersectionIsNormalInH: forall h: G2; forall n: G2;
  inH(h) -> (inH(n) and inNormal(n)) ->
  (inH(opG2(opG2(h, n), invG2(h))) and inNormal(opG2(opG2(h, n), invG2(h))))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall h: G2; forall n: G2;
      inH(h) -> (inH(n) and inNormal(n)) ->
      (inH(opG2(opG2(h, n), invG2(h))) and inNormal(opG2(opG2(h, n), invG2(h))))
    [using import(subgroup_product) theIntersectionIsNormalInH]
qed

// and both subgroups sit inside HN, which his third paragraph needs
theorem hSitsInsideTheProduct: forall h: G2; inH(h) -> inProduct(h)
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall h: G2; inH(h) -> inProduct(h)
    [using import(subgroup_product) hSitsInsideTheProduct]
qed

theorem nSitsInsideTheProduct: forall n: G2; inNormal(n) -> inProduct(n)
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall n: G2; inNormal(n) -> inProduct(n)
    [using import(subgroup_product) nSitsInsideTheProduct]
qed
```

What remains of this theorem is its $\cong$ step: Judson finishes by applying the First
Isomorphism Theorem to $\phi : H \rightarrow HN/N$, $h \mapsto hN$, whose kernel is
$\{h \in H : h \in N\} = H \cap N$. That chaining is an instance of the First theorem
at a particular map, and it concludes with the relation that cannot be named.

## Correspondence Theorem

**Theorem 11.13 (Correspondence Theorem).** Let $N$ be a normal subgroup of a group
$G$. Then $H \mapsto H/N$ is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of subgroups
$H$ of $G$ containing $N$ and the set of subgroups of $G/N$. Furthermore, the normal
subgroups of $G$ containing $N$ correspond to normal subgroups of $G/N$.

*Proof (Judson).* "Let $H$ be a subgroup of $G$ containing $N$. Since $N$ is normal in
$H$, $H/N$ is a factor group. Let $aN$ and $bN$ be elements of $H/N$. Then
$(aN)(b^{-1}N) = ab^{-1}N \in H/N$; hence, $H/N$ is a subgroup of $G/N$. Let $S$ be a
subgroup of $G/N$. … If $H = \{g \in G : gN \in S\}$, then … $H$ must be a subgroup of
$G$. Clearly, $H$ contains $N$. Therefore, $S = H/N$. … Suppose that $H_1$ and $H_2$
are subgroups of $G$ containing $N$ such that $H_1/N = H_2/N$. … However, since $N$ is
contained in $H_2$, we know that $h_1 \in H_2$." $\blacksquare$

**"$S = H/N$" is extensional agreement of two predicates on $G/N$** — which is what set
equality *means* when a subgroup is a predicate, and why the surjectivity result below
is an `iff`. Each half is stated at a *given* subgroup, which is what makes it an
instance of the correspondence rather than the correspondence itself.

```2b4m
import correspondence <<< "std/group/correspondence.b4m"

pred inHsub = correspondence.inHsub
pred inS = correspondence.inS
define inHoverN(q) = correspondence.inHoverN(q)
define inPullback(g) = correspondence.inPullback(g)
define inPullbackOverN(q) = correspondence.inPullbackOverN(q)

// FORWARD: H/N is a subgroup of G/N
theorem hOverNHasTheIdentity: inHoverN(EQ)
proof
  @conclusion |
    inHoverN(EQ)
    [using import(correspondence) hOverNHasTheIdentity]
qed

theorem hOverNIsClosedUnderTheOperation: forall x: Quot; forall y: Quot;
  inHoverN(x) -> inHoverN(y) -> inHoverN(opQ(x, y))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall x: Quot; forall y: Quot; inHoverN(x) -> inHoverN(y) -> inHoverN(opQ(x, y))
    [using import(correspondence) hOverNIsClosedUnderTheOperation]
qed

theorem hOverNIsClosedUnderInverses: forall x: Quot;
  inHoverN(x) -> inHoverN(invQ(x))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall x: Quot; inHoverN(x) -> inHoverN(invQ(x))
    [using import(correspondence) hOverNIsClosedUnderInverses]
qed

// BACKWARD: the pullback {g : gN in S} is a subgroup of G containing N
theorem thePullbackIsClosedUnderTheOperation: forall a: G; forall b: G;
  inPullback(a) -> inPullback(b) -> inPullback(opG(a, b))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall a: G; forall b: G; inPullback(a) -> inPullback(b) -> inPullback(opG(a, b))
    [using import(correspondence) thePullbackIsClosedUnderTheOperation]
qed

theorem thePullbackContainsN: forall g: G; inN(g) -> inPullback(g)
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall g: G; inN(g) -> inPullback(g)
    [using import(correspondence) thePullbackContainsN]
qed

// SURJECTIVITY: that pullback's image IS S
theorem thePullbacksImageIsS: forall q: Quot; inPullbackOverN(q) iff inS(q)
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall q: Quot; inPullbackOverN(q) iff inS(q)
    [using import(correspondence) thePullbacksImageIsS]
qed

// INJECTIVITY: equal images force extensionally equal subgroups. This is the step
// where "H contains N" is load-bearing -- Judson's "since N is contained in H_2, we
// know that h_1 in H_2".
theorem equalImagesMakeTheSubgroupsEqual:
  (forall q: Quot; inHoverN(q) iff correspondence.inH2overN(q)) ->
  forall g: G; inHsub(g) iff correspondence.inHsub2(g)
proof
  @conclusion |
    (forall q: Quot; inHoverN(q) iff correspondence.inH2overN(q)) ->
      forall g: G; inHsub(g) iff correspondence.inHsub2(g)
    [using import(correspondence) equalImagesMakeTheSubgroupsEqual]
qed
```

**A prediction recorded and then corrected.** When this chapter was first surveyed I
recorded that the Correspondence Theorem would be "the one theorem
stated-but-not-transliterated", reasoning that it quantifies over *subgroups* of $G/N$
and a subgroup is a predicate with no sort of predicates to range over. Probing settled
it: all of the content proves, including the injectivity step. The error was reasoning
about what `std` *has* rather than what the language can *express* — the same error
`CHAPTER9-PAINPOINTS` already records three times. See
`drafts/chapter11-probes/README`.

## Third Isomorphism Theorem

**Theorem 11.14 (Third Isomorphism Theorem).** Let $G$ be a group and $N$ and $H$ be
normal subgroups of $G$ with $N \subset H$. Then
$$G/H \cong \frac{G/N}{H/N}.$$

Judson gives **no proof** — it is left to the reader, and the Correspondence Theorem's
last paragraph sketches the route ($G \rightarrow G/N \rightarrow (G/N)/(H/N)$ has
kernel $H$).

This is not transliterated. It needs two *nested* quotients — $G/N$, then
$(G/N)/(H/N)$ — which means instantiating `std/group/factor.b4m` twice, the second time
with the first quotient as its ambient group. That is a model of the quotient theory
onto its own output, and `std/integer/mod-n.b4m`'s header records the live limitation
it would meet: the quotient template composes over a *standalone* substrate, but over a
quotient substrate it hits the one-source-theory mapping restriction. So the obstacle
here is in the **model system**, not in the mathematics — and unlike the $\cong$ limit
it is a thing to fix rather than a thing to state.

## What this section establishes

| result | content | $\cong$ step |
|---|---|---|
| canonical homomorphism | proved | — |
| First Isomorphism | $\eta$ well-defined, a homomorphism, injective | not nameable |
| Second Isomorphism | $HN$ a subgroup, $H \cap N$ normal in $H$ | not nameable |
| Correspondence | both directions, surjectivity, injectivity | not nameable |
| Third Isomorphism | not transliterated — needs nested quotients | — |

The $\cong$ column is one limit, recorded once in §9.1 and unchanged since. The Third
theorem's gap is different in kind: a model-system restriction with a known shape.
