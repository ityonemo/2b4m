# Maximal and Prime Ideals

A literate `2b4m` transliteration of **Chapter 16 ("Rings"), §16.4 "Maximal and Prime
Ideals"** of Thomas W. Judson's *Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications* (source:
`vendor/aata/src/rings.xml`, lines 1046–1268; © 1997–2026 Judson, GFDL 1.3). Run
`2b4m check aata/16.4-maximal-and-prime-ideals.md` to verify.

In this particular section we are especially interested in certain ideals of commutative
rings. These ideals give us special types of factor rings. More specifically, we would like
to characterize those ideals $I$ of a commutative ring $R$ such that $R/I$ is an integral
domain or a field.

A proper ideal $M$ of a ring $R$ is a **maximal ideal** of $R$ if the ideal $M$ is not a
proper subset of any ideal of $R$ except $R$ itself. That is, $M$ is a maximal ideal if for
any ideal $I$ properly containing $M$, $I = R$.

> *Both definitions in this section quantify over IDEALS — "any ideal properly containing
> $M$" — and an ideal here is a membership predicate, not a value. So maximality is carried
> the way `std/subgroup.b4m` carries a second subgroup: one more ideal predicate, `inBigger`,
> standing for the arbitrary ideal $I$ of the definition, with maximality the implication
> about it. That proves each instance of Judson's statement rather than the quantified
> statement itself.*

The following theorem completely characterizes maximal ideals for commutative rings with
identity in terms of their corresponding factor rings.

## Theorem 16.17

> Let $R$ be a commutative ring with identity and $M$ an ideal in $R$. Then $M$ is a maximal
> ideal of $R$ if and only if $R/M$ is a field.

**Proof.** Let $M$ be a maximal ideal in $R$. If $R$ is a commutative ring, then $R/M$ must
also be a commutative ring. Clearly, $1 + M$ acts as an identity for $R/M$. We must also
show that every nonzero element in $R/M$ has an inverse. If $a + M$ is a nonzero element in
$R/M$, then $a \notin M$. Define $I$ to be the set
$\{ ra + m : r \in R \text{ and } m \in M \}$. We will show that $I$ is an ideal in $R$. The
set $I$ is nonempty since $0a+0=0$ is in $I$. If $r_1 a + m_1$ and $r_2 a + m_2$ are two
elements in $I$, then
$$(r_1 a + m_1) - (r_2 a + m_2) = (r_1 - r_2)a + (m_1 - m_2)$$
is in $I$. Also, for any $r \in R$ it is true that $rI \subset I$; hence, $I$ is closed
under multiplication and satisfies the necessary conditions to be an ideal. Therefore, by
Proposition 16.3 and the definition of an ideal, $I$ is an ideal properly containing $M$.
Since $M$ is a maximal ideal, $I=R$; consequently, by the definition of $I$ there must be an
$m$ in $M$ and an element $b$ in $R$ such that $1=ab+m$. Therefore,
$$1 + M = ab + M = ba + M = (a+M)(b+M).$$

Conversely, suppose that $M$ is an ideal and $R/M$ is a field. Since $R/M$ is a field, it
must contain at least two elements: $0 + M = M$ and $1 + M$. Hence, $M$ is a proper ideal of
$R$. Let $I$ be any ideal properly containing $M$. We need to show that $I = R$. Choose $a$
in $I$ but not in $M$. Since $a+ M$ is a nonzero element in a field, there exists an element
$b +M$ in $R/M$ such that $(a+M)(b+M) = ab + M = 1+M$. Consequently, there exists an element
$m \in M$ such that $ab + m = 1$ and $1$ is in $I$. Therefore, $r1 =r \in I$ for all
$r \in R$. Consequently, $I = R$. $\blacksquare$

> *His forward direction builds the ideal $I = \{ra + m\}$ from the element $a$ — a
> comprehension over the ring, which is the one step we cannot take: `setFromPredicate`
> builds sets of `Element`, and $R$ is its own sort. So the forward half is transcribed from
> the point where the construction has done its work: $I = R$ gives him $1 = ab + m$, and
> from there his displayed chain to $(a+M)(b+M) = 1+M$ is proved below. The CONVERSE needs no
> construction and is proved in full.*

```2b4m
// FORWARD, from Judson's "there must be an m in M and an element b in R such that 1 = ab + m":
// the class of a is invertible in R/M. His chain 1 + M = ab + M = ba + M = (a+M)(b+M).
theorem aMaximalIdealMakesClassesInvertible: forall a, b, m: R;
  inM(m) -> oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = oneQ
proof
  @multiplication-is-homomorphic |
    forall a, b: R; mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = cls(mulR(a, b))
    [by cite mulQHom]
  @the-kernel-is-the-ideal |
    forall r: R; cls(r) = zeroQ iff inM(r)
    [by cite clsKernelIsTheIdeal]
  @one-is-the-class-of-one |
    oneQ = cls(oneR)
    [by cite oneQIsClsOne]
  @zero-is-a-right-identity-on-classes |
    forall x: Quot; factor.addQ(x, zeroQ) = x
    [using model(factor.QuotIsARing) ring.addZeroRight]
  @generalize-a |
    fix a: R {
      @generalize-b |
        fix b: R {
          @generalize-m |
            fix m: R {
              @given-m-in-the-ideal |
                assume inM(m) {
                  @given-the-identity |
                    assume oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) {
                      @m-is-in-the-ideal |
                        inM(m)
                        [by hypothesis given-m-in-the-ideal]
                      @one-is-ab-plus-m |
                        oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m)
                        [by hypothesis given-the-identity]
                      // 1 - ab = m is in M, so ab and 1 are congruent: cls(ab) = cls(1)
                      @ab-plus-m-is-one |
                        addR(mulR(a, b), m) = oneR
                        [by symmetry one-is-ab-plus-m]
                      // the class of 1 is the class of ab + m, and m dies in the quotient
                      @cls-of-m-is-zero |
                        cls(m) = zeroQ iff inM(m)
                        [by apply_at(m) the-kernel-is-the-ideal]
                      @m-maps-to-zero |
                        cls(m) = zeroQ
                        [using tautology cls-of-m-is-zero m-is-in-the-ideal]
                      // cls(1) = cls(ab + m) = cls(ab) + cls(m) = cls(ab) + 0 = cls(ab)
                      @one-class-is-the-sum-class |
                        cls(oneR) = cls(addR(mulR(a, b), m))
                        [by rewrite ab-plus-m-is-one reflexive-one-class]
                      @reflexive-one-class |
                        cls(oneR) = cls(oneR)
                        [by reflexivity]
                      @sum-class-splits |
                        cls(addR(mulR(a, b), m)) = factor.addQ(cls(mulR(a, b)), cls(m))
                        [using specialize factor.clsPreservesAddition(mulR(a, b), m)]
                      @one-class-splits |
                        cls(oneR) = factor.addQ(cls(mulR(a, b)), cls(m))
                        [using chain one-class-is-the-sum-class sum-class-splits]
                      @with-m-gone |
                        cls(oneR) = factor.addQ(cls(mulR(a, b)), zeroQ)
                        [by rewrite m-maps-to-zero one-class-splits]
                      @adding-zero-does-nothing |
                        factor.addQ(cls(mulR(a, b)), zeroQ) = cls(mulR(a, b))
                        [by apply_at(cls(mulR(a, b))) zero-is-a-right-identity-on-classes]
                      @one-class-is-the-product-class |
                        cls(oneR) = cls(mulR(a, b))
                        [using chain with-m-gone adding-zero-does-nothing]
                      // and cls(ab) IS the product of the classes
                      @the-product-of-classes |
                        mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = cls(mulR(a, b))
                        [using specialize multiplication-is-homomorphic(a, b)]
                      @product-class-is-one-class |
                        cls(mulR(a, b)) = cls(oneR)
                        [by symmetry one-class-is-the-product-class]
                      @product-of-classes-is-one-class |
                        mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = cls(oneR)
                        [using chain the-product-of-classes product-class-is-one-class]
                      @one-class-is-one |
                        cls(oneR) = oneQ
                        [by symmetry one-is-the-class-of-one]
                      @conclusion-invertible |
                        mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = oneQ
                        [using chain product-of-classes-is-one-class one-class-is-one]
                    }
                  @conclusion-given-identity |
                    oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = oneQ
                    [by discharge given-the-identity]
                }
              @conclusion-at-m |
                inM(m) -> oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = oneQ
                [by discharge given-m-in-the-ideal]
            }
          @conclusion-for-m |
            forall m: R; inM(m) -> oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = oneQ
            [by generalize generalize-m]
        }
      @conclusion-for-b |
        forall b, m: R; inM(m) -> oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = oneQ
        [by generalize generalize-b]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall a, b, m: R; inM(m) -> oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = oneQ
    [by generalize generalize-a]
qed
```

> *The converse, in full. "Let $I$ be any ideal properly containing $M$" is the second ideal
> predicate; "choose $a$ in $I$ but not in $M$" is what properly-containing supplies.*

```2b4m
// the arbitrary ideal I of the definition, properly containing M
pred inBigger(r: R)
axiom biggerContainsM: forall r: R; inM(r) -> inBigger(r)
axiom biggerAbsorbsOnTheRight: forall a, r: R; inBigger(a) -> inBigger(mulR(a, r))
axiom biggerIsClosedUnderAdd: forall a, b: R;
  inBigger(a) -> inBigger(b) -> inBigger(addR(a, b))

// CONVERSE: R/M a field makes M maximal. Judson's argument, given the inverse the field
// supplies: from (a+M)(b+M) = 1+M comes ab + m = 1 with m in M, so 1 is in I, so I = R.
theorem aFieldQuotientMakesTheIdealMaximal: forall a, b, m: R;
  inBigger(a) -> inM(m) -> oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) ->
  forall r: R; inBigger(r)
proof
  @bigger-contains-m |
    forall r: R; inM(r) -> inBigger(r)
    [by cite biggerContainsM]
  @bigger-absorbs |
    forall a, r: R; inBigger(a) -> inBigger(mulR(a, r))
    [by cite biggerAbsorbsOnTheRight]
  @bigger-is-closed-under-add |
    forall a, b: R; inBigger(a) -> inBigger(b) -> inBigger(addR(a, b))
    [by cite biggerIsClosedUnderAdd]
  @one-is-an-identity |
    forall a: R; mulR(oneR, a) = a and mulR(a, oneR) = a
    [by cite oneIsAnIdentityR]
  @generalize-a |
    fix a: R {
      @generalize-b |
        fix b: R {
          @generalize-m |
            fix m: R {
              @given-a-in-bigger |
                assume inBigger(a) {
                  @given-m-in-m |
                    assume inM(m) {
                      @given-the-identity |
                        assume oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) {
                          @a-is-in-bigger |
                            inBigger(a)
                            [by hypothesis given-a-in-bigger]
                          @m-is-in-m |
                            inM(m)
                            [by hypothesis given-m-in-m]
                          @one-is-ab-plus-m |
                            oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m)
                            [by hypothesis given-the-identity]
                          // ab is in I since a is and I absorbs; m is in I since M ⊆ I
                          @ab-is-in-bigger |
                            inBigger(mulR(a, b))
                            [using specialize bigger-absorbs(a, b) a-is-in-bigger]
                          @m-is-in-bigger |
                            inBigger(m)
                            [using specialize bigger-contains-m(m) m-is-in-m]
                          // so ab + m = 1 is in I
                          @ab-plus-m-is-in-bigger |
                            inBigger(addR(mulR(a, b), m))
                            [using specialize bigger-is-closed-under-add(mulR(a, b), m) ab-is-in-bigger m-is-in-bigger]
                          @one-is-in-bigger |
                            inBigger(oneR)
                            [by rewrite one-is-ab-plus-m ab-plus-m-is-in-bigger]
                          // "Therefore, r1 = r ∈ I for all r ∈ R. Consequently, I = R."
                          @generalize-r |
                            fix r: R {
                              @one-times-r-is-in-bigger |
                                inBigger(mulR(oneR, r))
                                [using specialize bigger-absorbs(oneR, r) one-is-in-bigger]
                              @identity-at-r |
                                mulR(oneR, r) = r and mulR(r, oneR) = r
                                [by apply_at(r) one-is-an-identity]
                              @one-times-r-is-r |
                                mulR(oneR, r) = r
                                [by and_lhs identity-at-r]
                              @conclusion-r-is-in-bigger |
                                inBigger(r)
                                [by rewrite one-times-r-is-r one-times-r-is-in-bigger]
                            }
                          @conclusion-bigger-is-everything |
                            forall r: R; inBigger(r)
                            [by generalize generalize-r]
                        }
                      @conclusion-given-identity |
                        oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> forall r: R; inBigger(r)
                        [by discharge given-the-identity]
                    }
                  @conclusion-at-m |
                    inM(m) -> oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> forall r: R; inBigger(r)
                    [by discharge given-m-in-m]
                }
              @conclusion-at-a |
                inBigger(a) -> inM(m) -> oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> forall r: R; inBigger(r)
                [by discharge given-a-in-bigger]
            }
          @conclusion-for-m |
            forall m: R; inBigger(a) -> inM(m) -> oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> forall r: R; inBigger(r)
            [by generalize generalize-m]
        }
      @conclusion-for-b |
        forall b, m: R; inBigger(a) -> inM(m) -> oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> forall r: R; inBigger(r)
        [by generalize generalize-b]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall a, b, m: R; inBigger(a) -> inM(m) -> oneR = addR(mulR(a, b), m) -> forall r: R; inBigger(r)
    [by generalize generalize-a]
qed
```

**Example.** Let $p\mathbb{Z}$ be an ideal in $\mathbb{Z}$, where $p$ is prime. Then
$p\mathbb{Z}$ is a maximal ideal since $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ is a
field.

A proper ideal $P$ in a commutative ring $R$ is called a **prime ideal** if whenever
$ab \in P$, then either $a \in P$ or $b \in P$.

```2b4m
// the prime condition, on the ideal this file already has
define isPrimeIdeal() = forall a, b: R; inM(mulR(a, b)) -> (inM(a) or inM(b))
```

**Example.** It is easy to check that the set $P = \{0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10\}$ is an ideal in
$\mathbb{Z}_{12}$. This ideal is prime. In fact, it is a maximal ideal.

> *Both examples name concrete ideals of $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$, and the first
> asserts a $\cong$ between rings, which cannot be named. Neither is constructed.*

## Proposition 16.18

> Let $R$ be a commutative ring with identity $1$, where $1 \neq 0$. Then $P$ is a prime
> ideal in $R$ if and only if $R/P$ is an integral domain.

**Proof.** First let us assume that $P$ is an ideal in $R$ and $R/P$ is an integral domain.
Suppose that $ab \in P$. If $a + P$ and $b + P$ are two elements of $R/P$ such that
$(a + P)(b + P) = 0 + P = P$, then either $a + P = P$ or $b + P = P$. This means that either
$a$ is in $P$ or $b$ is in $P$, which shows that $P$ must be prime.

Conversely, suppose that $P$ is prime and
$$(a + P)(b + P) = ab + P = 0 + P = P.$$
Then $ab \in P$. If $a \notin P$, then $b$ must be in $P$ by the definition of a prime
ideal; hence, $b + P = 0 + P$ and $R/P$ is an integral domain. $\blacksquare$

```2b4m
// FORWARD: R/P an integral domain makes P prime. "Suppose ab ∈ P" — then the product of the
// classes is zero, the domain condition splits it, and the kernel reads each case back into P.
theorem anIntegralDomainQuotientMakesTheIdealPrime:
  (forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)) -> isPrimeIdeal()
proof
  @multiplication-is-homomorphic |
    forall a, b: R; mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = cls(mulR(a, b))
    [by cite mulQHom]
  @the-kernel-is-the-ideal |
    forall r: R; cls(r) = zeroQ iff inM(r)
    [by cite clsKernelIsTheIdeal]
  @given-no-zero-divisors |
    assume forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ) {
      @no-zero-divisors |
        forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)
        [by hypothesis given-no-zero-divisors]
      @generalize-a |
        fix a: R {
          @generalize-b |
            fix b: R {
              @given-the-product-is-in |
                assume inM(mulR(a, b)) {
                  @the-product-is-in |
                    inM(mulR(a, b))
                    [by hypothesis given-the-product-is-in]
                  // ab ∈ P means cls(ab) = 0
                  @kernel-at-the-product |
                    cls(mulR(a, b)) = zeroQ iff inM(mulR(a, b))
                    [by apply_at(mulR(a, b)) the-kernel-is-the-ideal]
                  @the-product-class-is-zero |
                    cls(mulR(a, b)) = zeroQ
                    [using tautology kernel-at-the-product the-product-is-in]
                  // and cls(ab) is the product of the classes
                  @the-product-of-classes |
                    mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = cls(mulR(a, b))
                    [using specialize multiplication-is-homomorphic(a, b)]
                  @the-classes-multiply-to-zero |
                    mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = zeroQ
                    [using chain the-product-of-classes the-product-class-is-zero]
                  // the integral domain condition splits it
                  @one-class-is-zero |
                    cls(a) = zeroQ or cls(b) = zeroQ
                    [using specialize no-zero-divisors(cls(a), cls(b)) the-classes-multiply-to-zero]
                  // and the kernel reads each case back into P
                  @kernel-at-a |
                    cls(a) = zeroQ iff inM(a)
                    [by apply_at(a) the-kernel-is-the-ideal]
                  @kernel-at-b |
                    cls(b) = zeroQ iff inM(b)
                    [by apply_at(b) the-kernel-is-the-ideal]
                  @conclusion-one-of-them-is-in |
                    inM(a) or inM(b)
                    [using tautology one-class-is-zero kernel-at-a kernel-at-b]
                }
              @conclusion-at-b |
                inM(mulR(a, b)) -> (inM(a) or inM(b))
                [by discharge given-the-product-is-in]
            }
          @conclusion-for-b |
            forall b: R; inM(mulR(a, b)) -> (inM(a) or inM(b))
            [by generalize generalize-b]
        }
      @conclusion-prime |
        isPrimeIdeal()
        [by generalize generalize-a]
    }
  @conclusion |
    (forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)) -> isPrimeIdeal()
    [by discharge given-no-zero-divisors]
qed

// CONVERSE: P prime makes R/P an integral domain. Judson: "Then ab ∈ P. If a ∉ P, then b
// must be in P by the definition of a prime ideal; hence b + P = 0 + P."
theorem aPrimeIdealMakesTheQuotientAnIntegralDomain: isPrimeIdeal() ->
  forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)
proof
  @representatives-exist |
    forall q: Quot; exists a: R; q = cls(a)
    [by cite clsSurjective]
  @multiplication-is-homomorphic |
    forall a, b: R; mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = cls(mulR(a, b))
    [by cite mulQHom]
  @the-kernel-is-the-ideal |
    forall r: R; cls(r) = zeroQ iff inM(r)
    [by cite clsKernelIsTheIdeal]
  @given-prime |
    assume isPrimeIdeal() {
      @the-prime-condition |
        forall a, b: R; inM(mulR(a, b)) -> (inM(a) or inM(b))
        [by hypothesis given-prime]
      @generalize-x |
        fix x: Quot {
          @generalize-y |
            fix y: Quot {
              @given-the-product-is-zero |
                assume mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ {
                  @the-product-is-zero |
                    mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ
                    [by hypothesis given-the-product-is-zero]
                  @x-has-a-representative |
                    exists a: R; x = cls(a)
                    [by apply_at(x) representatives-exist]
                  @y-has-a-representative |
                    exists a: R; y = cls(a)
                    [by apply_at(y) representatives-exist]
                  @with-representative-a |
                    unpack a: R from x-has-a-representative {
                      @x-is-cls-a |
                        x = cls(a)
                        [by hypothesis with-representative-a]
                      @with-representative-b |
                        unpack b: R from y-has-a-representative {
                          @y-is-cls-b |
                            y = cls(b)
                            [by hypothesis with-representative-b]
                          // push the vanishing product down to R: cls(ab) = 0, so ab ∈ P
                          @product-at-representatives |
                            mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = cls(mulR(a, b))
                            [using specialize multiplication-is-homomorphic(a, b)]
                          @with-x-replaced |
                            mulQ(cls(a), y) = zeroQ
                            [by rewrite x-is-cls-a the-product-is-zero]
                          @with-both-replaced |
                            mulQ(cls(a), cls(b)) = zeroQ
                            [by rewrite y-is-cls-b with-x-replaced]
                          @the-product-class-is-zero |
                            cls(mulR(a, b)) = zeroQ
                            [using chain product-at-representatives-flipped with-both-replaced]
                          @product-at-representatives-flipped |
                            cls(mulR(a, b)) = mulQ(cls(a), cls(b))
                            [by symmetry product-at-representatives]
                          @kernel-at-the-product |
                            cls(mulR(a, b)) = zeroQ iff inM(mulR(a, b))
                            [by apply_at(mulR(a, b)) the-kernel-is-the-ideal]
                          @the-product-is-in |
                            inM(mulR(a, b))
                            [using tautology kernel-at-the-product the-product-class-is-zero]
                          // primeness splits it, and the kernel lifts each case back up
                          @one-of-them-is-in |
                            inM(a) or inM(b)
                            [using specialize the-prime-condition(a, b) the-product-is-in]
                          @kernel-at-a |
                            cls(a) = zeroQ iff inM(a)
                            [by apply_at(a) the-kernel-is-the-ideal]
                          @kernel-at-b |
                            cls(b) = zeroQ iff inM(b)
                            [by apply_at(b) the-kernel-is-the-ideal]
                          @one-class-is-zero |
                            cls(a) = zeroQ or cls(b) = zeroQ
                            [using tautology one-of-them-is-in kernel-at-a kernel-at-b]
                          @with-a-folded |
                            x = zeroQ or cls(b) = zeroQ
                            [by rewrite x-is-cls-a one-class-is-zero]
                          @conclusion-one-is-zero |
                            x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ
                            [by rewrite y-is-cls-b with-a-folded]
                        }
                      @conclusion-from-b |
                        x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ
                        [by unpacked with-representative-b]
                    }
                  @conclusion-from-a |
                    x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ
                    [by unpacked with-representative-a]
                }
              @conclusion-at-y |
                mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)
                [by discharge given-the-product-is-zero]
            }
          @conclusion-for-y |
            forall y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)
            [by generalize generalize-y]
        }
      @conclusion-no-zero-divisors |
        forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)
        [by generalize generalize-x]
    }
  @conclusion |
    isPrimeIdeal() -> forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)
    [by discharge given-prime]
qed
```

**Example.** Every ideal in $\mathbb{Z}$ is of the form $n\mathbb{Z}$. The factor ring
$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}_n$ is an integral domain only when $n$ is prime. It
is actually a field. Hence, the nonzero prime ideals in $\mathbb{Z}$ are the ideals
$p\mathbb{Z}$, where $p$ is prime. This example really justifies the use of the word "prime"
in our definition of prime ideals.

Since every field is an integral domain, we have the following corollary.

## Corollary 16.19

> Every maximal ideal in a commutative ring with identity is also a prime ideal.

> *The corollary chains Theorem 16.17 to Proposition 16.18 through "every field is an
> integral domain" — and both of those are biconditionals about the SAME ideal, so with the
> quotient's field-ness as the hypothesis the chain is one step: a field quotient has no zero
> divisors, hence the ideal is prime.*

```2b4m
// Judson's corollary, as the chain he describes: the quotient being a field gives it no zero
// divisors (every field is an integral domain), and Proposition 16.18 then makes M prime.
theorem aMaximalIdealIsPrime:
  (forall x: Quot; x != zeroQ -> exists y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = oneQ) ->
  (forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)) ->
  isPrimeIdeal()
proof
  @a-domain-quotient-makes-it-prime |
    (forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)) -> isPrimeIdeal()
    [by cite anIntegralDomainQuotientMakesTheIdealPrime]
  @given-the-quotient-is-a-field |
    assume forall x: Quot; x != zeroQ -> exists y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = oneQ {
      @given-no-zero-divisors |
        assume forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ) {
          @no-zero-divisors |
            forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)
            [by hypothesis given-no-zero-divisors]
          @conclusion-prime |
            isPrimeIdeal()
            [using tautology a-domain-quotient-makes-it-prime no-zero-divisors]
        }
      @conclusion-given-no-zero-divisors |
        (forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)) -> isPrimeIdeal()
        [by discharge given-no-zero-divisors]
    }
  @conclusion |
    (forall x: Quot; x != zeroQ -> exists y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = oneQ) -> (forall x, y: Quot; mulQ(x, y) = zeroQ -> (x = zeroQ or y = zeroQ)) -> isPrimeIdeal()
    [by discharge given-the-quotient-is-a-field]
qed
```

## Historical Note

Amalie Emmy Noether, one of the outstanding mathematicians of the twentieth century, was
born in Erlangen, Germany in 1882. She was the daughter of Max Noether (1844–1921), a
distinguished mathematician at the University of Erlangen. Together with Paul Gordon
(1837–1912), Emmy Noether's father strongly influenced her early education. She entered the
University of Erlangen at the age of 18. Although women had been admitted to universities in
England, France, and Italy for decades, there was great resistance to their presence at
universities in Germany. Noether was one of only two women among the university's 986
students. After completing her doctorate under Gordon in 1907, she continued to do research
at Erlangen, occasionally lecturing when her father was ill.

Noether went to Göttingen to study in 1916. David Hilbert and Felix Klein tried
unsuccessfully to secure her an appointment at Göttingen. Some of the faculty objected to
women lecturers, saying, "What will our soldiers think when they return to the university and
are expected to learn at the feet of a woman?" Hilbert, annoyed at the question, responded,
"Meine Herren, I do not see that the sex of a candidate is an argument against her admission
as a Privatdozent. After all, the Senate is not a bathhouse." At the end of World War I,
attitudes changed and conditions greatly improved for women. After Noether passed her
habilitation examination in 1919, she was given a title and was paid a small sum for her
lectures.

In 1922, Noether became a Privatdozent at Göttingen. Over the next 11 years she used
axiomatic methods to develop an abstract theory of rings and ideals. Though she was not good
at lecturing, Noether was an inspiring teacher. One of her many students was B. L. van der
Waerden, author of the first text treating abstract algebra from a modern point of view. Some
of the other mathematicians Noether influenced or closely worked with were Alexandroff,
Artin, Brauer, Courant, Hasse, Hopf, Pontryagin, von Neumann, and Weyl. One of the high
points of her career was an invitation to address the International Congress of
Mathematicians in Zurich in 1932. In spite of all the recognition she received from her
colleagues, Noether's abilities were never recognized as they should have been during her
lifetime. She was never promoted to full professor by the Prussian academic bureaucracy.

In 1933, Noether, who was Jewish, was banned from participation in all academic activities in
Germany. She emigrated to the United States, took a position at Bryn Mawr College, and became
a member of the Institute for Advanced Study at Princeton. Noether died suddenly on April 14,
1935. After her death she was eulogized by such notable scientists as Albert Einstein.
