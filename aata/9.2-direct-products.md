## External direct products

**Definition.** Let $G$ and $H$ be groups. The **external direct product**
$G \times H$ is the set of ordered pairs $(g, h)$ with $g \in G$ and $h \in H$, under
the componentwise operation
$$(g_1, h_1)(g_2, h_2) = (g_1 g_2,\ h_1 h_2).$$

**Proposition 9.13.** $G \times H$ is a group under the componentwise operation.

**Proof.** The operation is associative in each coordinate because it is associative in
$G$ and in $H$; $(e_G, e_H)$ is an identity; and $(g^{-1}, h^{-1})$ inverts $(g, h)$.
$\blacksquare$

## The order of a pair, and of a tuple

**Theorem 9.16.** Let $(g, h) \in G \times H$. If $g$ and $h$ have finite orders $r$ and
$s$ respectively, then the order of $(g, h)$ in $G \times H$ is the least common multiple of
$r$ and $s$.

**Proof.** Suppose that $m$ is the least common multiple of $r$ and $s$ and let
$n = |(g,h)|$. Then
$$\begin{aligned}(g,h)^m &= (g^m, h^m) = (e_G,e_H)\\ (g^n, h^n) &= (g, h)^n = (e_G,e_H).\end{aligned}$$
Hence, $n$ must divide $m$, and $n \leq m$. However, by the second equation, both $r$ and
$s$ must divide $n$; therefore, $n$ is a common multiple of $r$ and $s$. Since $m$ is the
*least common multiple* of $r$ and $s$, $m \leq n$. Consequently, $m$ must be equal to $n$.
$\blacksquare$

**Corollary 9.17.** Let $(g_1, \ldots, g_n) \in \prod G_i$. If $g_i$ has finite order
$r_i$ in $G_i$, then the order of $(g_1, \ldots, g_n)$ in $\prod G_i$ is the least
common multiple of $r_1, \ldots, r_n$.

> *Judson gives the corollary no proof. The formal development below establishes the pair
> case, which is his Theorem 9.16, and the divisibility characterization it rests on.*

A pair is the identity exactly when both components are:

```2b4m
theorem pairIsIdentityIffComponentsAre: forall p: Pair;
  p = prodE iff (fst(p) = EG and snd(p) = EH)
proof
  @generalize-p |
    fix p: Pair {
      @identity-is-the-pair-of-identities |
        prodE = pair(EG, EH)
        [by cite prodEDef]
      @p-is-its-projections |
        pair(fst(p), snd(p)) = p
        [using specialize pairExtensionality(p)]
      // forward: p = prodE makes each projection the corresponding identity
      @given-p-is-the-identity |
        assume p = prodE {
          @p-is-the-identity |
            p = prodE
            [by hypothesis given-p-is-the-identity]
          @p-is-the-identity-pair |
            p = pair(EG, EH)
            [using chain p-is-the-identity identity-is-the-pair-of-identities]
          @first-projection-of-the-identity-pair |
            fst(pair(EG, EH)) = EG
            [using specialize fstPair(EG, EH)]
          @second-projection-of-the-identity-pair |
            snd(pair(EG, EH)) = EH
            [using specialize sndPair(EG, EH)]
          @first-projection-is-eg |
            fst(p) = EG
            [by rewrite p-is-the-identity-pair first-projection-of-the-identity-pair]
          @second-projection-is-eh |
            snd(p) = EH
            [by rewrite p-is-the-identity-pair second-projection-of-the-identity-pair]
          @conclusion-both-components |
            fst(p) = EG and snd(p) = EH
            [by both first-projection-is-eg second-projection-is-eh]
        }
      @forward |
        p = prodE -> (fst(p) = EG and snd(p) = EH)
        [by discharge given-p-is-the-identity]
      // backward: both projections at the identities rebuild prodE by extensionality
      @given-both-components |
        assume fst(p) = EG and snd(p) = EH {
          @both-components |
            fst(p) = EG and snd(p) = EH
            [by hypothesis given-both-components]
          @first-is-eg |
            fst(p) = EG
            [by and_lhs both-components]
          @second-is-eh |
            snd(p) = EH
            [by and_rhs both-components]
          @rebuilt-with-eg |
            pair(EG, snd(p)) = p
            [by rewrite first-is-eg p-is-its-projections]
          @rebuilt-with-both |
            pair(EG, EH) = p
            [by rewrite second-is-eh rebuilt-with-eg]
          @identity-rebuilt |
            prodE = p
            [using chain identity-is-the-pair-of-identities rebuilt-with-both]
          @conclusion-p-is-the-identity |
            p = prodE
            [by symmetry identity-rebuilt]
        }
      @backward |
        (fst(p) = EG and snd(p) = EH) -> p = prodE
        [by discharge given-both-components]
      @conclusion-iff |
        p = prodE iff (fst(p) = EG and snd(p) = EH)
        [by make_equivalence forward backward]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall p: Pair; p = prodE iff (fst(p) = EG and snd(p) = EH)
    [by generalize generalize-p]
qed
```

And for a general exponent, $(g,h)^k$ is the identity precisely when $g^k$ and $h^k$ both
are. The componentwise power law $(g,h)^k = (g^k, h^k)$ is proved by induction on $k$ in
`std`; here is the consequence the two equations above use.

And his squeeze follows. Taking the component orders in the form "$g^k = e$ exactly when
$r \mid k$", the two directions give $r \mid k$ and $s \mid k$ together, which is
$\operatorname{lcm}(r,s) \mid k$ — minimality by `lcmLeast` one way, and
$r, s \mid \operatorname{lcm}(r,s)$ with transitivity the other.

> NB: This theorem exists in std in `std/group/product.b4m:560:pairPowerIsIdentityIffTheLcmDivides`.

```2b4m
theorem pairPowerIsIdentityIffTheLcmDivides: forall p: Pair; forall r, s: Int;

  (forall k: Int; powerH(snd(p), k) = EH iff divides(s, k)) ->
  forall k: Int; pairPower(p, k) = prodE iff divides(lcm(r, s), k)
proof
  @generalize-p |
    fix p: Pair {
      @generalize-r |
        fix r: Int {
          @generalize-s |
            fix s: Int {
              @given-first-order |
                assume forall k: Int; powerG(fst(p), k) = EG iff divides(r, k) {
                  @given-second-order |
                    assume forall k: Int; powerH(snd(p), k) = EH iff divides(s, k) {
                      @first-order |
                        forall k: Int; powerG(fst(p), k) = EG iff divides(r, k)
                        [by hypothesis given-first-order]
                      @second-order |
                        forall k: Int; powerH(snd(p), k) = EH iff divides(s, k)
                        [by hypothesis given-second-order]

                        fix k: Int {

                            powerG(fst(p), k) = EG iff divides(r, k)
                            [using specialize first-order(k)]

                            [using specialize second-order(k)]

                            [using tautology both-divide lcm-divides-if-both lcm-gives-both]

## Theorem 9.21 — when a product of cyclic groups is cyclic

**Theorem 9.21.** The group $\mathbb{Z}_m \times \mathbb{Z}_n$ is isomorphic to
$\mathbb{Z}_{mn}$ if and only if $\gcd(m,n) = 1$.

**Proof.** Suppose first that $\gcd(m,n) = 1$ and let $\operatorname{lcm}(m,n) = mn$.
By Theorem 9.17 the element $(1,1)$ has order $mn$, so
$\mathbb{Z}_m \times \mathbb{Z}_n$ is cyclic of order $mn$, hence isomorphic to
$\mathbb{Z}_{mn}$. Conversely, if $\gcd(m,n) = d > 1$ then $mn/d$ is divisible by both
$m$ and $n$, so by Theorem 9.17 every element $(a,b)$ satisfies
$(a,b) + \cdots + (a,b) = (0,0)$ with $mn/d$ summands. No element has order $mn$, so the
product is not cyclic. $\blacksquare$

> *Each side of this statement is a varying modulus $n$, and our $\mathbb{Z}_n$ has a
> single fixed modulus, so a family indexed by $n$ — and the isomorphism between two
> members of it — has no form here.*

**Example.** $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_6$, because $(1,1)$
generates the product — unlike $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, which is not
isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_4$.

## Internal direct products

The external direct product builds a large group out of two smaller groups. We would
like to reverse this process and say when a group is isomorphic to the direct product of
two of its subgroups.

**Definition.** Let $G$ be a group with subgroups $H$ and $K$ satisfying

- $G = HK = \{ hk : h \in H, k \in K \}$;
- $H \cap K = \{ e \}$;
- $hk = kh$ for all $h \in H$ and $k \in K$.

Then $G$ is the **internal direct product** of $H$ and $K$.

**Example.** The group $U(8)$ is the internal direct product of $H = \{1, 3\}$ and
$K = \{1, 5\}$. The dihedral group $D_6$ is an internal direct product of
$H = \{1, r^3\}$ and $K = \{1, r^2, r^4, s, r^2 s, r^4 s\}$; since $K \cong S_3$, this
gives $D_6 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times S_3$. Not every group factors this way: were
$S_3$ the internal direct product of proper subgroups $H$ and $K$, one of them — say
$H$ — would have order 3, hence be $\{(1), (123), (132)\}$, and $K$ would have order 2;
but for no such $K$ does $hk = kh$ hold throughout.

**Theorem 9.24.** Let $G$ be the internal direct product of subgroups $H$ and $K$. Then
$G$ is isomorphic to $H \times K$.

**Proof.** Since $G$ is an internal direct product, we can write any $g \in G$ as
$g = hk$ with $h \in H$ and $k \in K$. Define $\phi : G \rightarrow H \times K$ by
$\phi(g) = (h, k)$.

The first problem is to show $\phi$ is well defined; that is, that $h$ and $k$ are
uniquely determined by $g$. Suppose $g = hk = h'k'$. Then $h^{-1}h' = k(k')^{-1}$ lies
in both $H$ and $K$, so it must be the identity. Therefore $h = h'$ and $k = k'$.

To show $\phi$ preserves the group operation, let $g_1 = h_1 k_1$ and $g_2 = h_2 k_2$
and observe that
$$\phi(g_1 g_2) = \phi(h_1 k_1 h_2 k_2) = \phi(h_1 h_2 k_1 k_2) = (h_1 h_2, k_1 k_2)
= (h_1, k_1)(h_2, k_2) = \phi(g_1)\phi(g_2).$$
That $\phi$ is one-to-one and onto is left as an exercise. $\blacksquare$

The two halves he does prove are statements about $G$ and the membership predicates of
$H$ and $K$. First, well-definedness — the decomposition $g = hk$ is unique:

// the two remaining conditions of an internal direct product: the intersection is
// trivial, and the two subgroups commute elementwise
axiom intersectionIsTrivial: forall g: Grp; inH(g) -> inK(g) -> g = E
axiom subgroupsCommute: forall h, k: Grp; inH(h) -> inK(k) -> op(h, k) = op(k, h)

theorem decompositionIsUnique: forall h, k, h2, k2: Grp;
  inH(h) -> inK(k) -> inH(h2) -> inK(k2) ->
  op(h, k) = op(h2, k2) -> (h = h2 and k = k2)
proof
  @generalize-h |
    fix h: Grp {
      @generalize-k |
        fix k: Grp {
          @generalize-h2 |
            fix h2: Grp {
              @generalize-k2 |
                fix k2: Grp {
                  @given-h-in-h |
                    assume inH(h) {
                      @given-k-in-k |
                        assume inK(k) {
                          @given-h2-in-h |
                            assume inH(h2) {
                              @given-k2-in-k |
                                assume inK(k2) {
                                  @given-products-agree |
                                    assume op(h, k) = op(h2, k2) {
                                      @h-in-h |
                                        inH(h)
                                        [by hypothesis given-h-in-h]
                                      @k-in-k |
                                        inK(k)
                                        [by hypothesis given-k-in-k]
                                      @h2-in-h |
                                        inH(h2)
                                        [by hypothesis given-h2-in-h]
                                      @k2-in-k |
                                        inK(k2)
                                        [by hypothesis given-k2-in-k]
                                      @products-agree |
                                        op(h, k) = op(h2, k2)
                                        [by hypothesis given-products-agree]
                                      // h⁻¹h' = k(k')⁻¹ : rearrange h·k = h'·k'
                                      @h-inverse-in-h |
                                        inH(inverse(h))
                                        [using specialize hClosedInverse(h) h-in-h]
                                      @k2-inverse-in-k |
                                        inK(inverse(k2))
                                        [using specialize kClosedInverse(k2) k2-in-k]
                                      @cross-element-in-h |
                                        inH(op(inverse(h), h2))
                                        [using specialize hClosedOp(inverse(h), h2) h-inverse-in-h h2-in-h]
                                      @cross-element-in-k |
                                        inK(op(k, inverse(k2)))
                                        [using specialize kClosedOp(k, inverse(k2)) k-in-k k2-inverse-in-k]
                                      // h⁻¹·(h·k)·k'⁻¹ collapses to k·k'⁻¹ on one side
                                      // and to h⁻¹·h' on the other
                                      @left-side-regrouped |
                                        op(inverse(h), op(op(h, k), inverse(k2))) =
                                          op(op(inverse(h), h), op(k, inverse(k2)))
                                        [using assoc(opAssoc)]
                                      @h-inverse-cancels-h |
                                        op(inverse(h), h) = E
                                        [using specialize opInverseLeft(h)]
                                      @left-side-loses-h |
                                        op(inverse(h), op(op(h, k), inverse(k2))) =
                                          op(E, op(k, inverse(k2)))
                                        [by rewrite h-inverse-cancels-h left-side-regrouped]
                                      @identity-on-the-left-side |
                                        op(E, op(k, inverse(k2))) = op(k, inverse(k2))
                                        [using specialize opIdentityLeft(op(k, inverse(k2)))]
                                      @left-side-is-the-k-cross-element |
                                        op(inverse(h), op(op(h, k), inverse(k2))) =
                                          op(k, inverse(k2))
                                        [using chain left-side-loses-h identity-on-the-left-side]
                                      @right-side-regrouped |
                                        op(inverse(h), op(op(h2, k2), inverse(k2))) =
                                          op(op(inverse(h), h2), op(k2, inverse(k2)))
                                        [using assoc(opAssoc)]
                                      @k2-cancels-its-inverse |
                                        op(k2, inverse(k2)) = E
                                        [using specialize opInverseRight(k2)]
                                      @right-side-loses-k2 |
                                        op(inverse(h), op(op(h2, k2), inverse(k2))) =
                                          op(op(inverse(h), h2), E)
                                        [by rewrite k2-cancels-its-inverse right-side-regrouped]
                                      @identity-on-the-right-side |
                                        op(op(inverse(h), h2), E) = op(inverse(h), h2)
                                        [using specialize opIdentityRight(op(inverse(h), h2))]
                                      @right-side-is-the-h-cross-element |
                                        op(inverse(h), op(op(h2, k2), inverse(k2))) =
                                          op(inverse(h), h2)
                                        [using chain right-side-loses-k2 identity-on-the-right-side]
                                      // the two dressed-up sides are the same term
                                      @both-dressings-agree |
                                        op(inverse(h), op(op(h, k), inverse(k2))) =
                                          op(inverse(h), op(op(h2, k2), inverse(k2)))
                                        [by rewrite products-agree dressing-reflexivity]
                                      @dressing-reflexivity |
                                        op(inverse(h), op(op(h, k), inverse(k2))) =
                                          op(inverse(h), op(op(h, k), inverse(k2)))
                                        [by reflexivity]
                                      @the-k-cross-element-is-the-h-one |
                                        op(k, inverse(k2)) = op(inverse(h), h2)
                                        [using chain left-side-is-the-k-cross-element-flipped both-dressings-agree right-side-is-the-h-cross-element]
                                      @left-side-is-the-k-cross-element-flipped |
                                        op(k, inverse(k2)) =
                                          op(inverse(h), op(op(h, k), inverse(k2)))
                                        [by symmetry left-side-is-the-k-cross-element]
                                      @the-two-cross-elements-agree |
                                        op(inverse(h), h2) = op(k, inverse(k2))
                                        [by symmetry the-k-cross-element-is-the-h-one]
                                      @cross-element-in-k-too |
                                        inK(op(inverse(h), h2))
                                        [by rewrite the-two-cross-elements-agree cross-element-in-k]
                                      @cross-element-is-the-identity |
                                        op(inverse(h), h2) = E
                                        [using specialize intersectionIsTrivial(op(inverse(h), h2)) cross-element-in-h cross-element-in-k-too]
                                      @both-left-products-agree |
                                        op(inverse(h), h2) = op(inverse(h), h)
                                        [using chain cross-element-is-the-identity h-inverse-cancels-h-flipped]
                                      @h-inverse-cancels-h-flipped |
                                        E = op(inverse(h), h)
                                        [by symmetry h-inverse-cancels-h]
                                      @h2-equals-h |
                                        h2 = h
                                        [using specialize cancelLeft(inverse(h), h2, h) both-left-products-agree]
                                      @first-components-agree |
                                        h = h2
                                        [by symmetry h2-equals-h]
                                      // and then cancel h on the left of h·k = h'·k'
                                      @products-agree-at-h |
                                        op(h, k) = op(h, k2)
                                        [by rewrite first-components-agree products-agree]
                                      @second-components-agree |
                                        k = k2
                                        [using specialize cancelLeft(h, k, k2) products-agree-at-h]
                                      @conclusion-components-agree |
                                        h = h2 and k = k2
                                        [by both first-components-agree second-components-agree]
                                    }
                                  @products-agree-implies |
                                    op(h, k) = op(h2, k2) -> (h = h2 and k = k2)
                                    [by discharge given-products-agree]
                                }
                              @k2-implies |
                                inK(k2) -> op(h, k) = op(h2, k2) -> (h = h2 and k = k2)
                                [by discharge given-k2-in-k]
                            }
                          @h2-implies |
                            inH(h2) -> inK(k2) -> op(h, k) = op(h2, k2) -> (h = h2 and k = k2)
                            [by discharge given-h2-in-h]
                        }
                      @k-implies |
                        inK(k) -> inH(h2) -> inK(k2) -> op(h, k) = op(h2, k2) ->
                          (h = h2 and k = k2)
                        [by discharge given-k-in-k]
                    }
                  @h-implies |
                    inH(h) -> inK(k) -> inH(h2) -> inK(k2) -> op(h, k) = op(h2, k2) ->
                      (h = h2 and k = k2)
                    [by discharge given-h-in-h]
                }
              @discharge-k2 |
                forall k2: Grp; inH(h) -> inK(k) -> inH(h2) -> inK(k2) ->
                  op(h, k) = op(h2, k2) -> (h = h2 and k = k2)
                [by generalize generalize-k2]
            }
          @discharge-h2 |
            forall h2, k2: Grp; inH(h) -> inK(k) -> inH(h2) -> inK(k2) ->
              op(h, k) = op(h2, k2) -> (h = h2 and k = k2)
            [by generalize generalize-h2]
        }
      @discharge-k |
        forall k, h2, k2: Grp; inH(h) -> inK(k) -> inH(h2) -> inK(k2) ->
          op(h, k) = op(h2, k2) -> (h = h2 and k = k2)
        [by generalize generalize-k]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall h, k, h2, k2: Grp; inH(h) -> inK(k) -> inH(h2) -> inK(k2) ->
      op(h, k) = op(h2, k2) -> (h = h2 and k = k2)
    [by generalize generalize-h]
qed
```

And second, operation-preservation: in $h_1 k_1 h_2 k_2 = h_1 h_2 k_1 k_2$ the middle
$k_1 h_2$ swaps by the commuting condition, and the components then collect into
$(h_1 h_2, k_1 k_2)$.

```2b4m
theorem productsRecombineComponentwise: forall h1, k1, h2, k2: Grp;
  inH(h2) -> inK(k1) ->
  op(op(h1, k1), op(h2, k2)) = op(op(h1, h2), op(k1, k2))
proof
  @generalize-h1 |
    fix h1: Grp {
      @generalize-k1 |
        fix k1: Grp {
          @generalize-h2 |
            fix h2: Grp {
              @generalize-k2 |
                fix k2: Grp {
                  @given-h2-in-h |
                    assume inH(h2) {
                      @given-k1-in-k |
                        assume inK(k1) {
                          @h2-in-h |
                            inH(h2)
                            [by hypothesis given-h2-in-h]
                          @k1-in-k |
                            inK(k1)
                            [by hypothesis given-k1-in-k]
                          @middle-pair-commutes |
                            op(h2, k1) = op(k1, h2)
                            [using specialize subgroupsCommute(h2, k1) h2-in-h k1-in-k]
                          @middle-pair-commutes-flipped |
                            op(k1, h2) = op(h2, k1)
                            [by symmetry middle-pair-commutes]
                          // flatten both sides to h1·(k1·(h2·k2)) and h1·(h2·(k1·k2))
                          @left-side-flattened |
                            op(op(h1, k1), op(h2, k2)) = op(h1, op(k1, op(h2, k2)))
                            [using assoc(opAssoc)]
                          @right-side-flattened |
                            op(op(h1, h2), op(k1, k2)) = op(h1, op(h2, op(k1, k2)))
                            [using assoc(opAssoc)]
                          @middle-regrouped |
                            op(k1, op(h2, k2)) = op(op(k1, h2), k2)
                            [using assoc(opAssoc)]
                          @middle-swapped |
                            op(k1, op(h2, k2)) = op(op(h2, k1), k2)
                            [by rewrite middle-pair-commutes-flipped middle-regrouped]
                          @middle-reflattened |
                            op(op(h2, k1), k2) = op(h2, op(k1, k2))
                            [using assoc(opAssoc)]
                          @middle-is-the-right-middle |
                            op(k1, op(h2, k2)) = op(h2, op(k1, k2))
                            [using chain middle-swapped middle-reflattened]
                          @left-side-after-the-swap |
                            op(op(h1, k1), op(h2, k2)) = op(h1, op(h2, op(k1, k2)))
                            [by rewrite middle-is-the-right-middle left-side-flattened]
                          @conclusion-recombined |
                            op(op(h1, k1), op(h2, k2)) = op(op(h1, h2), op(k1, k2))
                            [using chain left-side-after-the-swap right-side-flattened-flipped]
                          @right-side-flattened-flipped |
                            op(h1, op(h2, op(k1, k2))) = op(op(h1, h2), op(k1, k2))
                            [by symmetry right-side-flattened]
                        }
                      @k1-implies |
                        inK(k1) ->
                          op(op(h1, k1), op(h2, k2)) = op(op(h1, h2), op(k1, k2))
                        [by discharge given-k1-in-k]
                    }
                  @h2-implies |
                    inH(h2) -> inK(k1) ->
                      op(op(h1, k1), op(h2, k2)) = op(op(h1, h2), op(k1, k2))
                    [by discharge given-h2-in-h]
                }
              @discharge-k2 |
                forall k2: Grp; inH(h2) -> inK(k1) ->
                  op(op(h1, k1), op(h2, k2)) = op(op(h1, h2), op(k1, k2))
                [by generalize generalize-k2]
            }
          @discharge-h2 |
            forall h2, k2: Grp; inH(h2) -> inK(k1) ->
              op(op(h1, k1), op(h2, k2)) = op(op(h1, h2), op(k1, k2))
            [by generalize generalize-h2]
        }
      @discharge-k1 |
        forall k1, h2, k2: Grp; inH(h2) -> inK(k1) ->
          op(op(h1, k1), op(h2, k2)) = op(op(h1, h2), op(k1, k2))
        [by generalize generalize-k1]
    }
  @conclusion |
    forall h1, k1, h2, k2: Grp; inH(h2) -> inK(k1) ->
      op(op(h1, k1), op(h2, k2)) = op(op(h1, h2), op(k1, k2))
    [by generalize generalize-h1]
qed
```

> groups here to carry $\cong$, and no sort of subgroups to quantify $H$ and $K$ over —
> they appear as membership predicates on $G$.*
