# The Multiplicative Group of Complex Numbers

A literate `2b4m` transliteration of **Chapter 4 ("Cyclic Groups"), §4.2 "The
Multiplicative Group of Complex Numbers"** of Thomas W. Judson's *Abstract
Algebra: Theory and Applications* (source: `vendor/aata/src/cyclic.xml`; ©
1997–2026 Judson, GFDL 1.3). The book's prose is reproduced in order. The
**algebraic** heart — complex arithmetic, the conjugate, and the modulus with its
multiplicative law — is formalized with machine-checked proofs; the **analytic**
material (polar form, DeMoivre's theorem, the roots of unity) needs trigonometry
that `2b4m` does not yet have, and is transliterated as prose with an in-place note
saying exactly what it would require. Run `2b4m check
aata/4.2-complex-multiplicative-group.md` to verify.

The complex numbers live in `std/complex.b4m` (ℂ as a field with the imaginary
unit $i$, an embedding of ℝ, real/imaginary parts, and conjugation), and the
modulus in `std/complex/modulus.b4m`. By Chapter 4 the reals and the field
axioms are in hand, so these are imported as prerequisites; the modulus law
$|zw| = |z|\,|w|$ (Exercise 19f) is proved in `std/complex/modulus.b4m` and cited
here.

## Complex arithmetic

The **complex numbers** are defined as
$$\mathbb{C} = \{ a + bi : a, b \in \mathbb{R} \},$$
where $i^2 = -1$. If $z = a + bi$, then $a$ is the **real part** of $z$ and $b$
is the **imaginary part**. To add, we add corresponding parts:
$$z + w = (a + bi) + (c + di) = (a + c) + (b + d)i;$$
and remembering $i^2 = -1$, we multiply like polynomials:
$$(a + bi)(c + di) = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)i.$$
Every nonzero $z = a + bi$ has a multiplicative inverse
$$z^{-1} = \frac{a - bi}{a^2 + b^2},$$
so $\mathbb{C}^* = \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$ is a group under multiplication.
The **complex conjugate** of $z = a + bi$ is $\overline{z} = a - bi$, and the
**absolute value** or **modulus** is $|z| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.

These are exactly the field structure of `std/complex.b4m` ($i^2 = -1$, the ℝ
embedding, `re`/`im`/`conj`) together with the modulus of
`std/complex/modulus.b4m`; the group $\mathbb{C}^*$ is that field's nonzero
elements under multiplication.

## The modulus and its properties

The key structural facts about the modulus — the ones §4.2 uses and Exercise 19
asks to prove — are that $z\overline{z} = |z|^2$, that conjugation preserves the
modulus, and that the modulus is **multiplicative**, $|zw| = |z|\,|w|$. The last
is what makes the circle group $\mathbb{T} = \{ z : |z| = 1 \}$ a group. Each is
proved in `std/complex/modulus.b4m` (from the projection algebra of ℂ, with no
trigonometry); we state them here in the book's notation.

$z \overline{z} = |z|^2$ (as an embedded real, $|z|^2 = \mathrm{re}(z)^2 +
\mathrm{im}(z)^2$):

```2b4m
theorem conjugateProduct: forall z: C; mul(z, conj(z)) = complex.fromReal(normSq(z))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall z: C; mul(z, conj(z)) = complex.fromReal(normSq(z))
    [using import(complex_modulus) zTimesConjIsNormSq]
qed
```

Conjugation preserves the modulus, $|\overline{z}| = |z|$ (Exercise 19a):

```2b4m
theorem modulusOfConjugate: forall z: C; abs(conj(z)) = abs(z)
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall z: C; abs(conj(z)) = abs(z)
    [using import(complex_modulus) absConj]
qed
```

The modulus is multiplicative, $|zw| = |z|\,|w|$ (Exercise 19f):

```2b4m
theorem modulusMultiplicative: forall z, w: C; abs(mul(z, w)) = real.mul(abs(z), abs(w))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall z, w: C; abs(mul(z, w)) = real.mul(abs(z), abs(w))
    [using import(complex_modulus) absMul]
qed
```

## Polar form and DeMoivre's theorem

The polar representation of a complex number makes products and powers easy.
Writing $z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta) = r\operatorname{cis}\theta$ with $r =
|z|$, one has, for $z = r\operatorname{cis}\theta$ and $w =
s\operatorname{cis}\phi$,
$$zw = rs\operatorname{cis}(\theta + \phi),$$
and **DeMoivre's Theorem**: for a nonzero $z = r\operatorname{cis}\theta$,
$$[r\operatorname{cis}\theta]^n = r^n\operatorname{cis}(n\theta), \quad n = 1, 2,
\ldots,$$
proved by induction on $n$ using the angle-addition formulas for sine and cosine.

The trigonometry this needs — $\sin$, $\cos$ and $\pi$ with the angle-addition
formulas and $\cos^2 + \sin^2 = 1$ — is adjoined to $\mathbb{R}$ axiomatically in
`std/real/trig.b4m` (as $\sqrt{\ }$ is), and $n\theta$ is the natural multiple
$\theta + \cdots + \theta$ of `std/real/scaling.b4m`. With $\operatorname{cis}\theta =
\cos\theta + i\sin\theta$ and $z^n$ the natural power on $\mathbb{C}$, the product rule is
complex extensionality plus the addition formulas, and DeMoivre is induction on $n$
(`std/complex/polar.b4m`).

```2b4m
import complex_polar <<< "std/complex/polar.b4m"
import real_scaling <<< "std/real/scaling.b4m"
import peano <<< "std/peano.b4m"

define cis(t) = complex_polar.cis(t)
func cpow = complex_polar.cpow
func scale = real_scaling.scale
sort Nat = peano.Nat

// cis θ · cis φ = cis(θ + φ), and so (r cis θ)(s cis φ) = rs cis(θ + φ).
theorem cisProduct: forall a, b: real.Real; mul(cis(a), cis(b)) = cis(real.add(a, b))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall a, b: real.Real; mul(cis(a), cis(b)) = cis(real.add(a, b))
    [using import(complex_polar) cisAdd]
qed

theorem polarProduct: forall r, s, a, b: real.Real; mul(mul(complex.fromReal(r), cis(a)), mul(complex.fromReal(s), cis(b))) = mul(complex.fromReal(real.mul(r, s)), cis(real.add(a, b)))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall r, s, a, b: real.Real; mul(mul(complex.fromReal(r), cis(a)), mul(complex.fromReal(s), cis(b))) = mul(complex.fromReal(real.mul(r, s)), cis(real.add(a, b)))
    [using import(complex_polar) polarProduct]
qed

// |cis θ| = 1.
theorem modulusOfCis: forall t: real.Real; abs(cis(t)) = real.ONE
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall t: real.Real; abs(cis(t)) = real.ONE
    [using import(complex_polar) absOfCis]
qed

// DeMoivre: (r cis θ)^n = r^n cis(nθ), with r^n the natural power on ℝ.
theorem deMoivre: forall r, t: real.Real; forall n: Nat; cpow(mul(complex.fromReal(r), cis(t)), n) = mul(complex.fromReal(real.pow(r, n)), cis(scale(n, t)))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall r, t: real.Real; forall n: Nat; cpow(mul(complex.fromReal(r), cis(t)), n) = mul(complex.fromReal(real.pow(r, n)), cis(scale(n, t)))
    [using import(complex_polar) deMoivrePolar]
qed
```

## The circle group and the roots of unity

The circle group is
$$\mathbb{T} = \{ z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| = 1 \},$$
a subgroup of $\mathbb{C}^*$ (a direct consequence of $|zw| = |z|\,|w|$, proved
above). Although $\mathbb{T}$ has infinite order it has many finite subgroups.
The complex numbers satisfying $z^n = 1$ are the **$n$th roots of unity**; by
DeMoivre's theorem they are
$$z = \operatorname{cis}\!\left(\frac{2k\pi}{n}\right), \quad k = 0, 1, \ldots, n
- 1,$$
and they form a cyclic subgroup of $\mathbb{T}$ of order $n$. A generator is a
**primitive $n$th root of unity**.

The circle group $\mathbb{T}$ itself — the predicate $|z| = 1$ — is expressible
once the modulus is in hand (it is defined above), and "$\mathbb{T}$ is a
subgroup" follows from `modulusMultiplicative` together with $|1| = 1$ and
$|z^{-1}| = |z|^{-1}$. But its finite subgroups, the roots of unity, are stated
via $\operatorname{cis}$, so they inherit the trigonometry gap:

With $\theta = 2\pi/n$ (which exists for every $n \ge 1$: $2\pi \cdot n^{-1}$), each
$\operatorname{cis}(k\theta)$ is an $n$-th root of unity, by DeMoivre:
$\operatorname{cis}(k\theta)^n = \operatorname{cis}(n \cdot k\theta) =
\operatorname{cis}(k \cdot 2\pi) = \operatorname{cis}(2\pi)^k = 1$. That these are *all*
the roots of $z^n = 1$ (so that the subgroup has order exactly $n$) forwards to "a
polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ roots", a later chapter, and to the injectivity
of $\operatorname{cis}$ on $[0, 2\pi)$, which the trigonometric axioms do not pin down;
those two facts are left in prose. The circle group $\mathbb{T} = \{z : |z| = 1\}$ is a
subgroup of $\mathbb{C}^*$: it contains $1$, is closed under products ($|zw| =
|z||w|$), and under inverses — on $\mathbb{T}$, $z^{-1} = \overline{z}/|z|^2 =
\overline{z}$.

```2b4m
define twoPi = real.twoPi
define onCircle(z) = complex_polar.onCircle(z)

theorem rootAngleExists: forall m: Nat; exists theta: real.Real; scale(peano.succ(m), theta) = twoPi
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall m: Nat; exists theta: real.Real; scale(peano.succ(m), theta) = twoPi
    [using import(complex_polar) rootAngleExists]
qed

theorem rootsOfUnity: forall n: Nat; forall theta: real.Real; scale(n, theta) = twoPi -> forall k: Nat; cpow(cis(scale(k, theta)), n) = complex.ONE
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall n: Nat; forall theta: real.Real; scale(n, theta) = twoPi -> forall k: Nat; cpow(cis(scale(k, theta)), n) = complex.ONE
    [using import(complex_polar) rootsOfUnity]
qed

theorem circleGroupHasOne: onCircle(complex.ONE)
proof
  @conclusion |
    onCircle(complex.ONE)
    [using import(complex_polar) circleHasOne]
qed

theorem circleGroupClosedUnderProduct: forall z, w: C; onCircle(z) -> onCircle(w) -> onCircle(mul(z, w))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall z, w: C; onCircle(z) -> onCircle(w) -> onCircle(mul(z, w))
    [using import(complex_polar) circleClosedUnderProduct]
qed

theorem circleGroupClosedUnderInverse: forall z: C; onCircle(z) -> onCircle(complex.recip(z))
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall z: C; onCircle(z) -> onCircle(complex.recip(z))
    [using import(complex_polar) circleClosedUnderInverse]
qed
```
