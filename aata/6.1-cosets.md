# Cosets

A literate `2b4m` transliteration of **Chapter 6 ("Cosets and Lagrange's Theorem"),
§6.1 "Cosets"** of Thomas W. Judson's *Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications*
(source: `vendor/aata/src/cosets.xml`, lines 25–244; © 1997–2026 Judson, GFDL 1.3).
The book's prose is reproduced in order — the definition of a left coset, the
equivalence lemma, the partition theorem, and the index — with a machine-checked
`2b4m` proof following each result. Run `2b4m check aata/6.1-cosets.md` to verify.

The coset theory lives in `std/group/coset.b4m`, layered on `std/subgroup.b4m` and
`std/group.b4m`. A coset is not a *set* here: `2b4m` has no set-of-group-elements
sort, so `gH` is represented by its **membership predicate** `inCoset(g, x)` —
"$x \in gH$". Set equality `g_1H = g_2H` is then extensional agreement of the two
predicates, which is exactly how the book uses it.

## Definition

**Definition.** Let $G$ be a group and $H$ a subgroup of $G$. Define a **left
coset** of $H$ with **representative** $g \in G$ to be the set
$$gH = \{ gh : h \in H \}.$$
**Right cosets** can be defined similarly by $Hg = \{ hg : h \in H \}$. If left and
right cosets coincide or if it is clear from the context to which type of coset we
are referring, we will use the word *coset* without specifying left or right.

We import the coset theory and alias it to the book's notation: the group $G$, the
subgroup-membership relation $H$ (writing `inH(h)` for "$h \in H$"), and the coset
membership $gH$ (writing `inCoset(g, x)` for "$x \in gH$").

```2b4m
import coset <<< "std/group/coset.b4m"

// the book's G, with its operation and inverses
sort G = coset.Grp
const e = coset.E
func op = coset.op
func inv = coset.inverse

// the book's H: a subgroup, as the membership predicate characterizing it
pred inH = coset.inSubgroup

// x ∈ gH
pred inCoset = coset.inCoset
```

In a commutative group, left and right cosets are always identical. It is not always
the case that a left coset is the same as a right coset — the book's $S_3$ examples
with $H = \{(1), (1\,2\,3), (1\,3\,2)\}$ and $K = \{(1), (1\,2)\}$ exhibit both
behaviours. Those are concrete computations in a specific group; the results below
are the general facts.

Every element lies in its own coset — the fact that makes the cosets *cover* $G$.

```2b4m
theorem elementIsInItsOwnCoset = coset.elementIsInItsOwnCoset
```

## Lemma 6.1 — the equivalence

**Lemma 6.1.** Let $H$ be a subgroup of a group $G$ and suppose that
$g_1, g_2 \in G$. The following conditions are equivalent.

1. $g_1 H = g_2 H$;
2. $H g_1^{-1} = H g_2^{-1}$;
3. $g_1 H \subset g_2 H$;
4. $g_2 \in g_1 H$;
5. $g_1^{-1} g_2 \in H$.

Judson leaves the proof as an exercise. The hinge of the cycle is the equivalence of
(4) and (5) — "$g_2$ lies in $g_1H$" iff "$g_1^{-1}g_2$ lies in $H$" — which is the
library's `inCosetIffQuotientIsInTheSubgroup`, and it is the form every later
argument actually uses.

```2b4m
theorem membershipIffQuotientIsInH = coset.inCosetIffQuotientIsInTheSubgroup
```

Conditions (1) and (4) are equivalent too: two cosets agree exactly when each
representative lies in the other's coset. Stated over the membership predicates,
"$g_1H = g_2H$" is `forall y: G; inCoset(g, y) iff inCoset(k, y)`.

```2b4m
theorem cosetsCoincideIffQuotientIsInH = coset.cosetsCoincideIffQuotientIsInTheSubgroup
```

## Theorem 6.2 — the cosets partition the group

**Theorem 6.2.** Let $H$ be a subgroup of a group $G$. Then the left cosets of $H$
in $G$ partition $G$. That is, the group $G$ is the disjoint union of the left
cosets of $H$ in $G$.

*Proof (Judson).* Let $g_1 H$ and $g_2 H$ be two cosets of $H$ in $G$. We must show
that either $g_1 H \cap g_2 H = \emptyset$ or $g_1 H = g_2 H$. Suppose that
$g_1 H \cap g_2 H \neq \emptyset$ and $a \in g_1 H \cap g_2 H$. Then by the
definition of a left coset, $a = g_1 h_1 = g_2 h_2$ for some elements $h_1$ and
$h_2$ in $H$. Hence, $g_1 = g_2 h_2 h_1^{-1}$ or $g_1 \in g_2 H$. By Lemma 6.1,
$g_1 H = g_2 H$. $\blacksquare$

That is exactly the library's statement: a shared element forces the two cosets to
agree everywhere. Together with `elementIsInItsOwnCoset` (every element is covered)
this is the partition.

```2b4m
theorem cosetsAreEqualOrDisjoint = coset.cosetsAreEqualOrDisjoint
```

There is nothing special in this theorem about left cosets. Right cosets also
partition $G$; the proof of this fact is exactly the same as the proof for left
cosets except that all group multiplications are done on the opposite side of $H$.

## The index

**Definition.** Let $G$ be a group and $H$ be a subgroup of $G$. Define the
**index** of $H$ in $G$ to be the number of left cosets of $H$ in $G$. We will
denote the index by $[G:H]$.

**Theorem 6.3.** Let $H$ be a subgroup of a group $G$. The number of left cosets of
$H$ in $G$ is the same as the number of right cosets of $H$ in $G$.

Counting cosets needs a notion of *how many*, and `2b4m` has no cardinality
function: a count here is a **listing** — a `Seq` whose entries below a length are
pairwise distinct and exhaust what is being counted. That machinery is what §6.2
builds on, so the index appears there rather than here. What §6.1 does supply is the
fact that makes every coset the *same* size: translation by $g$ maps $H$ onto $gH$
injectively, so a listing of $H$ of length $p$ yields a listing of $gH$ of length
$p$.

```2b4m
theorem everyCosetIsListedByTheSubgroupsLength = coset.everyCosetIsListedByTheSubgroupsLength
```

The two halves of that bijection are available separately — translation lands in the
coset and is injective, and every coset member is a translation.

```2b4m
theorem translationLandsInTheCoset = coset.translationLandsInTheCoset
theorem translationIsInjective = coset.translationIsInjective
theorem everyCosetMemberIsATranslation = coset.everyCosetMemberIsATranslation
```
