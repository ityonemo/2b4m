# Cyclic Subgroups (Exercises)

Exercises for Judson **§4.1 "Cyclic Subgroups"** (source:
`vendor/aata/src/cyclic.xml`; © 1997–2026 Judson, GFDL 1.3), following
`.claude/rules/aata-guide.md`. The exercises use `std` **liberally** — including
`std/cyclic.bpa`, `std/group-order.bpa`, and the concrete `std/integer-mod-n.bpa`
(ℤ_n / U(n)). Results the main text proves are **forwarded** (a prose pointer, no
re-proof); brute numeric computations are **skipped**; and results needing
machinery `bpa` does not yet have (an order-of-element *function* over an arbitrary
group, direct products, the torsion subgroup) are **Deferred** with the
construction named in place.

Comparing orders of different elements ($|a| = |a^{-1}|$, $|ab| = |ba|$) needs the
order stated as a first-class object over arbitrary elements. Rather than an order
*function* `ord : G → ℕ` (which would need a choice/definite-description operator
`bpa` lacks, to extract the least period), `std/group-order.bpa` provides the order
as a single-valued **relation** `hasOrder(g, n)` — "$n$ is the order of $g$" — with
`orderIsUnique` (an element has at most one order). This lets orders be stated and
compared with no new primitive, and is what Exercise 23(a) below is proved from.
Exercises still needing more (an order-of-product bound, direct products, the
torsion construction) remain Deferred with the missing piece named.

We import the group + order machinery, aliased to the book's notation.

```bpa
import group_power <<< "std/group-power.bpa"
import group_order <<< "std/group-order.bpa"

sort G = group_power.Grp
const e = group_power.E
func inv = group_power.inverse
func power = group_power.power
sort Int = group_power.Int
pred hasOrder = group_order.hasOrder
```

## Exercises

**1. Prove or disprove.** (a) All generators of $\mathbb{Z}_{60}$ are prime —
(b) $U(8)$ is cyclic — (c) $\mathbb{Q}$ is cyclic — (d) if every proper subgroup
of $G$ is cyclic then $G$ is cyclic — (e) a group with finitely many subgroups is
finite.

**Skipped / Deferred.** (a),(b),(c) are concrete numeric/structural claims about
$\mathbb{Z}_{60}$, $U(8)$, $\mathbb{Q}$ (a,b need the specific modeled instances +
enumeration; c needs $\mathbb{Q}$ as a group and a non-cyclicity argument) —
**skipped** as brute/concrete. (d) is **false** ($Q_8$, the quaternions) and (e)
is **true**, but both need finite-group / counting machinery — **Deferred** (finite
groups, subgroup enumeration).

**2. Orders of elements** ($5 \in \mathbb{Z}_{12}$, $\sqrt3 \in \mathbb{R}$, …).
**Skipped** — brute numeric order computations in specific concrete groups.

**3. List subgroup elements** (the subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ generated by 7, of
$\mathbb{Z}_{24}$ by 15, all subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$, …). **Skipped** —
concrete enumeration in specific groups.

**4. Subgroups of $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$ generated by given matrices.** **Deferred** —
needs a 2×2 real-matrix sort / $GL_2$.

**5–9.** Orders of every element in $\mathbb{Z}_{18}$ / $D_4$; cyclic subgroups of
$Q_8$; cyclic subgroups of $U(30)$; generators of the order-8 subgroup of
$\mathbb{Z}_{32}$. **Skipped / Deferred** — brute enumeration (5, 9) or the
concrete permutation/quaternion groups $D_4$, $Q_8$ and finite $U(30)$ (6, 7, 8).

**10. Elements of finite order in $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Q}^*$, $\mathbb{R}^*$.**
**Deferred** — needs the finite-order predicate over these concrete groups
(and $\mathbb{Q}^*$, $\mathbb{R}^*$ as multiplicative groups).

**11. If $a^{24} = e$, the possible orders of $a$.** **Deferred** — needs the
order-of-element function and the divisors of 24 as a computed set.

**12–22.** Cyclic groups with a prescribed number of generators; which $U(n)$ are
cyclic; matrix orders in $GL_2$; complex arithmetic / polar / roots-of-unity
computations; repeated-squares numerics. **Skipped / Deferred** — brute numeric
(15–18, 22), roots of unity / polar (16, 17, 20, 21 — need the trigonometry gap of
§4.2), or the concrete $U(n)$ / $GL_2$ structures (13, 14).

**23. Order relations.** (a) $|a| = |a^{-1}|$ — (b) $|a| = |g^{-1}ag|$ — (c) $|ab|
= |ba|$.

**(a) is proved.** Using the `hasOrder` relation: if $n$ is the order of $a$, then
$n$ is the order of $a^{-1}$. The proof (in `std/group-order.bpa` as
`inverseHasSameOrder`) observes $(a^{-1})^k = (a^k)^{-1}$, so $(a^{-1})^k = e
\iff a^k = e$ — hence $a^{-1}$ is killed by exactly the same exponents as $a$, so
they share both the killing period and its minimality.

```bpa
theorem inverseHasSameOrder: forall a: G; forall n: Int; hasOrder(a, n) -> hasOrder(inv(a), n)
proof
  @conclusion |
    forall a: G; forall n: Int; hasOrder(a, n) -> hasOrder(inv(a), n)
    [using import(group_order) inverseHasSameOrder]
qed
```

**(b), (c) Deferred.** $|a| = |g^{-1}ag|$ (conjugation preserves order) and $|ab| =
|ba|$ ($ab$, $ba$ are conjugate) are the same relational shape but need the
conjugation-is-a-power-preserving-map lemma $(g^{-1}ag)^k = g^{-1}a^k g$; reachable
from `hasOrder` with that added lemma.

**24, 25. Number of generators of $\mathbb{Z}_{pq}$ / $\mathbb{Z}_{p^r}$.**
**Deferred** — needs Euler's totient $\varphi$ (the generator count of $\mathbb{Z}_n$
is $\varphi(n)$) and its prime-power formula.

**26. $\mathbb{Z}_p$ has no nontrivial subgroups for $p$ prime.** **Deferred** —
needs $\mathbb{Z}_p$ modeled at a prime $p$ plus the subgroup-order-divides-group
argument (Lagrange-flavored); reachable once a prime instance of
`std/integer-mod-n.bpa` and a subgroup-order fact are in hand.

**27. $|\langle g \rangle \cap \langle h \rangle|$ for $|g| = 15$, $|h| = 16$.**
**Deferred** — needs finite-order intersection + $\gcd(15,16)=1 \Rightarrow$
trivial intersection (the Exercise 30 fact) plus cardinality.

**28. A generator for $\langle a^m \rangle \cap \langle a^n \rangle$.**
**Deferred** — the answer is $a^{\mathrm{lcm}(m,n)}$; needs lcm-indexed cyclic
subgroups and the intersection-of-cyclic-subgroups computation.

**29. $\mathbb{Z}_n$ has an even number of generators for $n > 2$.** **Deferred**
— pairs each generator $r$ with $n - r$ (also a generator, distinct for $n > 2$);
needs $\mathbb{Z}_n$'s generator set as a finite countable object.

**30. $\gcd(|a|,|b|) = 1 \Rightarrow \langle a \rangle \cap \langle b \rangle =
\{e\}$.** **Deferred** — with the `hasOrder` relation now available, the shape is in
reach: an element of the intersection has order dividing both $|a|$ and $|b|$ (via
`powerIsIdentityIffOrderDivides`), hence dividing $\gcd(|a|,|b|) = 1$, so its order
is 1 and it is $e$. What remains is the intersection membership plumbing (an element
in both $\langle a \rangle$ and $\langle b \rangle$) tied to the order-divides fact.

**31. The torsion subgroup** (elements of finite order in an abelian $G$ form a
subgroup). **Deferred** — needs the finite-order predicate over $G$ and the
order-of-product bound ($(gh)^{mn} = e$), i.e. an order function.

**32. In a cyclic group $G = \langle x \rangle$ of order $n$, if $y = x^k$ with
$\gcd(k,n) = 1$ then $y$ generates $G$.** **Forwarded** — this is the content of
the main-text theorem on the order of a power (§4.1, `powerOfPowerKills` /
`powerOfPowerMinimal`): when $d = \gcd(k,n) = 1$, the order of $y = x^k$ is $n/d =
n$, so $y$ has the same order as $x$ and thus generates $G$. See §4.1.

**33. An abelian $G$ with two order-2 cyclic subgroups has a subgroup of order 4.**
**Deferred** — needs the direct-product / internal-product construction and
finite-order counting.

**34. Abelian $G$ of order $pq$ ($\gcd(p,q)=1$) with elements of orders $p$, $q$ is
cyclic.** **Deferred** — needs the order-of-product law ($|ab| = |a|\,|b|$ when
$\gcd(|a|,|b|)=1$ in an abelian group) and cardinality.

**35. The subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}$ are exactly $n\mathbb{Z}$.** **Forwarded** —
proved in the main text of §4.1 (the corollary to "every subgroup of a cyclic group
is cyclic"). See §4.1.

**36. The generators of $\mathbb{Z}_n$ are the $r$ with $1 \le r < n$ and
$\gcd(r,n) = 1$.** **Forwarded** — proved in the main text of §4.1 (the corollary
to the order-of-a-power theorem). See §4.1.

**37. If $G$ has no proper nontrivial subgroups then $G$ is cyclic.** **Deferred**
— the argument (any $g \ne e$ has $\langle g \rangle = G$, else $\langle g \rangle$
is a proper nontrivial subgroup) needs quantification over the cyclic subgroup
$\langle g \rangle$ for a *variable* $g$; `std/cyclic.bpa` fixes its generator as a
const, so "for all $g$, $\langle g \rangle$ is a subgroup" is not directly
available. Reachable once cyclic subgroups are parameterized by the generator.

**38. The order of an element of a cyclic group divides the order of the group.**
**Forwarded** — immediate from the main-text §4.1 order results
(`powerIsIdentityIffOrderDivides`: $a^k = e \iff n \mid k$, applied to the element's
order). See §4.1.
